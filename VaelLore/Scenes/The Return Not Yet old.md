# Scene: The Return (Not Yet)

## Context

Occurs approximately two weeks after [The Leaving After](The_Leaving_After.md).

Involves:
- [Kael](Kael.md)
- [Saya](Saya.md)

---

## Scene

She found her in an abandoned relay station three districts out.

Kael heard the transport before it landed. She didn't move. She was sitting on the floor of what had been a communications room, her back against the wall, a half-assembled evidence file spread across the surface in front of her. She looked at the ceiling for a moment when she recognized the engine signature.

Then she looked back at the file.

---

Saya came alone, which was either trust or anger or both. She stepped through the door without announcing herself, which meant she had been watching the station long enough to know it was safe, which meant she had been planning this for at least a day, which meant it was not impulse.

Kael noted all of this.

She did not get up.

---

"You left evidence," Saya said.

"Yes."

"You didn't come back with it."

"No."

A pause. Saya looked at the file spread across the floor. At the careful way it was organized — each thread connected to the next, nothing floating loose. She looked at it for a long time.

"You're still building it."

"It's not airtight yet."

"It doesn't need to be airtight. I saw what you left. I understand what it means."

Kael said nothing.

---

Saya crossed the room.

She didn't sit. She stood in the way she stood when she was being an empress and not a sister — deliberate, grounded, the weight of the thing she carried visible in how still she held herself.

"I need you to tell me why you didn't come back."

"You exiled me."

"The exile—" Saya stopped. Started again. "I need to know why you didn't come back when you had something to bring me. Why you left it unsigned. Why you're still out here."

Another pause.

Longer this time.

---

"Because I needed to be sure."

"Of the evidence."

"Of myself."

Saya was quiet.

"I was right," Kael said. "About the threat. I was right and I still looked exactly like what you were afraid of. And I don't—" She stopped. "I don't know how to measure the distance between those two things anymore. I don't know how to tell you I can see clearly when I can't show you my sight. When the only instrument I have for checking my own calibration is the same one the Orokin cracked open."

She said this plainly. The way she said most things. No performance, no reaching for effect.

Just the truth, set down in the space between them.

"I'm not coming back until I know I'm safe to come back to."

---

Saya sat down.

Not across from her. Beside her. Close, in the way that only one person had ever been close to her without it requiring anything.

For a while neither of them spoke.

"I thought you were broken," Saya said finally. "I thought the Warframe, the experiments — I thought something had finally—"

"I know."

"I was wrong."

"You weren't entirely wrong." Kael looked at the evidence file. "That's what I'm telling you. You weren't wrong to ask the question. I'm asking it too."

Saya looked at her then.

Really looked.

The way she had always looked — not past the frame, not past the damage, not past whatever frightening thing was currently visible. Just at her.

"You killed our people," she said.

"Yes."

"And you were right."

"Yes."

"And you don't fully trust that."

A long pause.

"No."

---

Saya was quiet for a while after that.

The relay station settled around them — old structure, old silence, the Between moving through the walls the way it moved through everything out here on the edge of things.

"Come home," Saya said.

"Not yet."

"Kael."

"I need a little more time. I need the file to be complete. I need—" She stopped. "I need to know the answer to the question. I'm close."

"I'm not asking you to have the answer. I'm asking you to come home while you're still looking for it."

---

Kael looked at her sister.

At the gold at her collar and the red in her eyes and the specific quality of stillness that meant she had already decided and was waiting for Kael to catch up.

She thought about the Gold Anchor.

The thing it said.

*I come home.*

Not: *I come home when I am certain.*

Not: *I come home when I have proven myself.*

Just: *I come home.*

---

"Give me three days," Kael said.

It wasn't what Saya wanted.

It was what Kael had.

Saya looked at her for a long moment.

Then she nodded.

Once.

She stood, and crossed to the door, and stopped there with her back to the room.

"Three days," she said.

"Three days."

She left.

---

Kael sat in the empty relay station for a while after the transport sound faded.

She looked at the file.

She thought about what it meant to come home to something you had damaged. Not whether it was possible. She knew it was possible — she had done it before, in smaller ways, in the particular way that people who love each other do.

She thought about what it meant to come home still carrying the question.

Without the answer.

Without certainty.

She had always needed certainty before she moved.

She was beginning to understand that certainty, for her, had always been a way of protecting herself from having to be held.

---

She finished the file in two days.

She came home on the third.

---

## Notes

- Kael explains herself without the evidence as leverage — the truth is the only thing offered
- "I'm not coming back until I know I'm safe to come back to" — the question she's asking is about herself
- Saya doesn't accept the answer. She accepts the person asking.
- The Gold Anchor extends — *I come home* does not require certainty
- The scar: the question doesn't get answered fully. It gets carried home instead.
