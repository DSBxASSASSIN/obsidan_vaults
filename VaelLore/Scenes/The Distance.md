# Scene: The Distance

## Context

Occurs during the exile period. No single event — a compression of weeks.

Involves:
- [Kael](Kael.md)
- [Saya](Saya.md)

Two separate threads, not occurring simultaneously. They do not interact in this scene.

---

## Scene — Kael

She worked.

That was what she knew how to do when everything else became unavailable. She moved through the outer territories, the abandoned facilities, the edges of places no one was maintaining anymore, and she looked for anything that connected to the splinter group. Records. Contacts. Anyone who had known them before they were survivors.

She found fragments.

Not enough.

---

The Between was loud out here in a way it wasn't at home. No anchor. No voice at the edge of things to orient by. Just the deep current of it, moving through her in the places that had never fully healed, pulling her attention sideways into spaces that weren't quite this one.

She managed it.

She had always managed it.

But she noticed, in the quiet of empty corridors, that managing it was taking more than it used to. That the question she'd asked herself in the street outside the gate kept surfacing when she wasn't paying attention.

*What if she's right.*

Not about the threat. She still believed the threat. She had believed it before the calculation and she believed it after and the absence of evidence was not the same as the absence of truth.

But about the other thing.

About what the experiments had done to the membrane between her perception and reality. About whether what she heard through the Between was signal or distortion or some ratio of both that she was no longer equipped to calculate correctly.

She had been so certain.

She was still certain.

The certainty felt different now that she was looking directly at it.

---

She didn't reach for Saya.

Not because she didn't want to.

Because if she came back without proof she was asking Saya to choose between trusting her sister and trusting her own judgment, and she would not do that. She would not put that weight on her.

Not yet.

Not until she had something to hand her that didn't require faith.

---

## Scene — Saya

She ruled.

That was what she did. She had always ruled — it was the thing Kael had handed her and she had spent years learning to hold it properly — but she ruled now without the particular quality of presence that had always existed at the edge of rooms. A shape in the doorway. A stillness in the corridor outside. Something she had stopped consciously registering years ago because it had simply always been there.

It was not there now.

She noticed its absence the way you notice the absence of a sound you never knew you were listening for.

---

She was capable without it. She had always been capable. The work did not suffer — if anything, she was more visibly in command now, because there was nothing standing slightly behind her to suggest that command was shared or conditional. She made decisions alone and they were good decisions and her people adjusted to the new shape of things with a speed that surprised and did not comfort her.

She had been right to do it.

She told herself this regularly.

She was also not sleeping well, which she told no one.

---

She did not reach for Kael.

This was harder than she had expected.

She had sent people away before. She had made difficult decisions before. This was different in a way she kept circling without being able to name — something about the specific weight of sending away the person who had always, without exception, come home.

She thought about the Gold Anchor Failure sometimes in the dark.

The way Kael had looked when the disconnect hit.

The way she had caught her.

She thought about the silence in the courtyard.

The way Kael had not defended herself.

The way that silence could mean: *I have nothing to say in my defense.*

Or it could mean something else entirely, and she didn't know which, and Kael had not told her, and she was so tired of not knowing things about her sister that she had decided not to ask.

---

The grief didn't have a clean shape.

It was not grief for a death. It was grief for a certainty she had carried since childhood — that her sister was the one fixed point in every room she had ever stood in — and the loss of that certainty did not feel like freedom.

It felt like standing somewhere she had always assumed was solid and discovering it had always required something to hold it up.

She had not known.

That was the part that stayed with her.

She had not known what was holding her up until it was gone.

---

## Notes

- The distance costs both of them differently
- Kael's self-doubt is separate from her certainty about the threat — she holds both
- Saya's capability without Kael is real and not a comfort
- Neither reaches for the other. Neither is unaffected.
