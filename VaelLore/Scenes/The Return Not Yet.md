# Scene: The Return (Not Yet)

## Context

Occurs approximately two weeks after [The Leaving After](The_Leaving_After.md).

Involves:
- [Kael](Kael.md)
- [Saya](Saya.md)

---

## Scene

She found her in an abandoned relay station three districts out.

Kael heard the transport before it landed. She didn't move. She was sitting on the floor of what had been a communications room, her back against the wall, a half-assembled evidence file spread across the surface in front of her. She looked at the ceiling for a moment when she recognized the engine signature.

Then she looked back at the file.

---

The transport settled.

Nothing happened for a while after that.

Kael noted the silence. Noted how long it lasted. Noted what it meant that Saya had come alone, had found this place, had landed — and was still outside.

Waiting.

Making sure.

She understood what that was.

She let her sister take the time she needed.

---

Saya came through the door without announcing herself. She crossed the threshold and stopped, taking in the room — the file on the floor, the careful organization of it, Kael herself, sitting still against the wall with her hands loose in her lap.

She stood there for a moment.

Then she stepped inside.

---

"You left evidence," Saya said.

"Yes."

"You didn't come back with it."

"No."

A pause. Saya looked at the file spread across the floor. At the way each thread connected to the next, nothing floating loose, everything ordered with the particular precision of someone who had been doing this a long time and alone.

"You're still building it."

"It's not airtight yet."

"It doesn't need to be airtight. I saw what you left. I understand what it means." Saya's voice was steady. "I need you to tell me why you didn't come back."

"You exiled me."

"I need to know why you didn't come back when you had something to bring me. Why you left it unsigned. Why you're still out here."

---

Kael looked at the file for a moment.

Then she looked up.

"Because I needed to be sure."

"Of the evidence."

"Of myself."

Saya was quiet.

"I was right," Kael said. "About the threat. I was right and I still looked exactly like what you were afraid of. And I don't—" She stopped. "I don't know how to measure the distance between those two things anymore. I don't know how to tell you I can see clearly when I can't show you my sight. When the only instrument I have for checking my own calibration is the same one the Orokin cracked open."

She said this plainly.

The way she said most things.

---

Saya sat down.

Not across from her. Beside her. Close, in the way that only one person had ever been close to her without it requiring anything.

For a while neither of them spoke.

Then—

"I thought you were broken," Saya said. Her voice was quiet. Not cruel. Just honest, the way people are honest when they have been carrying something for weeks and the weight of it has finally made pretending impossible. "I thought — the Warframe, the experiments, everything they did to you — I thought something had finally given way. That I was finally seeing it."

The room went very still.

---

Kael's hands moved.

Not much. Just — up, briefly, to her face. Pressing once against her eyes. Coming back down.

She looked at the far wall.

Something in her expression had changed. Not collapsed — not yet. But the particular quality of control she had carried her entire life, the thing that had held through Orokin sessions and Warframe failures and six weeks of exile, had developed a fracture along a line Saya had not known existed.

"Kael—"

"I know you didn't mean it that way."

Her voice was steady.

Her hands moved again.

---

She pressed the back of her wrist against her eyes and held it there and Saya went very quiet beside her because she understood, suddenly, that she had done something she did not have the full shape of. That the words she had chosen — *broken, finally, they did to you* — had landed somewhere specific. Somewhere old.

She had heard language like that before.

Not from Saya.

From people who had looked at her the way you look at a thing you are trying to understand by taking apart.

*How far can we go before losing the subject.*

*She is more resilient than projected.*

*Document the deviation.*

She had never told Saya about the specific texture of those rooms. What they said while they worked. How clinical it was. How they spoke about her in the third person while she was still inside her own body, as if her presence in the room was already hypothetical.

Saya had not been there.

Saya did not know.

And she loved her sister completely and without condition and it did not matter — the words had still come from her mouth, in this quiet room, and Kael's hands would not stop moving to her face.

---

"I'm sorry," Saya said.

Not because she knew exactly what she had done.

Because she could see the shape of the damage even if she couldn't see its source.

Kael shook her head once. A small motion. *It's not your fault.* That was what it meant. It always meant that when she made that motion.

Which was somehow worse.

---

They sat with it for a while.

The relay station held the silence around them — old walls, old dark, the Between moving through everything the way it did out here at the edges. Kael's breathing evened out slowly. Her hands settled. Whatever had come loose inside her did not fully close again — she could feel the gap of it, the specific rawness of something exposed that had not been exposed before — but she brought herself back to the surface the way she always brought herself back.

Present.

Here.

Still her.

---

"I don't trust myself around you right now," she said finally.

Saya looked at her.

"Not because I would hurt you." Quickly. Clearly. "Not that. I mean—" She stopped. Started again more carefully. "You are the reason I come back. Every time. You are the thing I hold onto when the Between pulls and the frames go deep and everything else becomes—" She pressed her lips together. "If I come back and I'm still asking this question. Still not knowing if what I see can be trusted. I don't know what that does to the anchor. I don't know if I'm safe to lean on it when I'm not sure of myself."

A long pause.

"I don't want to damage the only thing that has ever brought me home."

---

Saya was quiet for a long time after that.

Long enough that the room settled fully around them.

Then she said: "You waited outside for me once."

Kael looked at her.

"The Gold Anchor Failure. You came back still in the frame and I heard it through the wall and I—" Saya's voice was even. Precise. "I stood outside your door for a moment before I came in. I have thought about that moment every day since."

Kael said nothing.

"I came in anyway." Saya held her gaze. "That's what I'm telling you."

---

Something shifted.

Not resolution. Not the clean closing of an open thing. Just — a degree of loosening. Enough that Kael could feel the edges of herself again, the way she could always feel them when Saya was close enough to be the anchor.

Still her.

Still here.

Still capable of coming home.

"Give me three days," she said.

It wasn't what Saya wanted.

It was what Kael had.

Saya looked at her for a long moment.

Then she nodded.

Once.

She stood, and crossed to the door, and stopped there with her back to the room.

"Three days," she said.

"Three days."

She left.

---

Kael sat in the empty relay station after the transport sound faded.

She didn't look at the file.

She sat with what had just happened — the specific shape of it, the fracture she hadn't known was there until it opened, the thing Saya had said that was not her fault and had broken something anyway.

She thought: *Saya waited outside.*

She thought: *She came in.*

She had always known her sister's love was unconditional. She had held it like a fact, like the Between, like something structural that did not require examination.

She had not known, until now, what it cost Saya to make it true.

She had not known it was a choice her sister made.

Every time.

---

She finished the file in two days.

On the third morning she sat for a while in the silence of the relay station before she moved.

Then she got up.

And went home.

---

## Notes

- Saya waiting outside the relay is the quiet scar — she knows she did it, and why, and can't undo the knowing
- Kael fractures not because Saya is cruel but because she isn't — the words came from love and still landed where the Orokin stood
- The tears Kael keeps wiping away — she doesn't perform emotion, so when it comes anyway it tells Saya everything she can't ask about
- Saya doesn't push into the wound she can't map. She offers her own instead.
- "She came in anyway" — the anchor reaffirmed not as certainty but as choice
- The scar: Saya now knows she waited. Kael now knows the anchor is a choice Saya makes, not a given. Both of them have to live with what that means.
