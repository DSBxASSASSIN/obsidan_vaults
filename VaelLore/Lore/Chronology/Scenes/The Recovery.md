# Scene: The Recovery

## Context

Occurs immediately after [[What She Said]]. Spans approximately six weeks — two weeks bedridden, four more returning to full duty.

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Sorin]]

---

## Scene

### The First Two Weeks

She came the first night without deciding to.

She had told herself she was checking on the medical report. She had the report. She read it in the corridor outside and then she went in anyway and sat in the chair beside his bed and stayed until the watch changed.

He was unconscious. She watched his breathing. She did not examine why she was watching it. She stayed until the watch changed and then she left.

She came back the next evening.

And the one after that.

---

She learned his schedule within the first few days. The hours when the medication kept him under properly, when the body had pulled him deep enough that there was no risk of him surfacing. She came in those hours. She sat. She left before the watch changed.

He never knew she was there.

That was the point, she told herself. He needed rest. She was checking on him. It was practical.

She sat in the chair beside his bed in the quiet hours and looked at his face and did not examine what she was doing there or why the chair felt like the right place to be or why leaving, each time, required more effort than arriving had.

She came every day for two weeks.

He never knew.

---

### The Return

He came back to light duty at the start of the fourth week.

She was not there when he returned to his post.

He had not expected her to be — it was not a formal occasion, not something that required her presence. He simply took his position at the edge of her rooms the way he always had and resumed the work of being where he was supposed to be.

She acknowledged him when she passed.

Professionally. Correctly. The nod she gave the guard when she had somewhere to be.

He filed it.

From his side the picture was clear: she had stayed with him the night of the surgery. Possibly said something — possibly. And then withdrawn completely. Professional. Correct. The guard and her charge, exactly as it was supposed to be.

The maybe-dream sat in the place where things too important to lose went to wait, and he could not reconcile it with what he was seeing, and so he told himself again that it had probably been a dream.

Almost certainly.

---

The weeks that followed had a particular quality.

They were both present. Both professional. Both doing exactly what the situation required — mission-critical information exchanged clearly, positions maintained correctly, the work of the relay continuing without interruption. Anyone watching would have seen nothing wrong.

Aldren, who had known them both long enough, saw the wrong thing immediately.

Not something present that shouldn't be. Something absent that should.

He said nothing yet.

He watched.

---

By the time Sorin was back on full duty the distance had set.

Not hostile. Not cold. Just — formal. Back to the beginning, almost. The ease that had built over years, the particular fluency of two people who had been in enough situations together to think in the same rhythm — gone quiet. She gave him information when information was required. He received it and acted on it. The work continued.

She did not come to sit quietly beside him anywhere.

He did not set cups of something hot beside her hand.

They were, in every visible way, exactly what they were supposed to be.

It was the most wrong either of them had felt in months and neither of them said so.

---

### What Sorin Carried

He was almost certain it was a dream.

He had been through surgery, blood loss, whatever they had given him for the pain. The soft place between sleep and waking was not reliable territory. He knew this. He was a practical man and practical men did not build things on the uncertain ground of half-heard words in a medical room at night.

Almost certain.

The problem was the specific quality of it. The way it had sounded like her — not a dream-version of her, not the soft-edged approximation the unconscious makes of people. Exactly her. The flatness when she said things she would have preferred not to say. The particular way she stopped and started when something was difficult. The specific economy of _so. That's the true thing._

He knew her voice.

He had catalogued it the way he catalogued everything about the people he had been trained to protect.

It sounded like her.

But she had been professional and correct since the moment he came back on duty, which was not the behavior of someone who had said what he thought he had heard. So either it was a dream, or it was real and she had decided to bury it, and he did not know which of those was harder to carry.

He carried both.

He said nothing.

He stood at his post and did the work and did not look at her differently than he always had, because looking at her differently would require having a reason and he did not know yet if he had one.
