## Scene

The splinter group arrived on the third day of the second month.

Eleven people. Eight adults, three children. Survivors of an Orokin auxiliary facility on the edge of charted space, brought in through Saya's contacts over the course of six careful weeks. Real work. Patient, methodical, the kind of recovery effort that required Saya to spend political capital she hadn't fully rebuilt yet.

She spent it.

They were Vael. It was not a question.

---

Kael was there when they came through the gate.

She stood back. She always stood back at arrivals — it was Saya's moment, not hers, and she understood the difference between a blade and a face. She watched from the corridor above, arms at her sides, still.

She watched the way you watch something you are trying to read.

Most of them moved the way survivors move — careful, a little collapsed inward, flinching at sounds that hadn't fully resolved into safe yet. The children moved differently, the way children always do, orbiting the adults in tight circuits. Two of the men carried themselves differently from the others. One woman near the back watched Saya with an attention that was too fixed, too still.

Kael noted it.

Filed it.

Said nothing.

---

Over the following weeks she watched more.

Not conspicuously. Not in a way anyone would have called surveillance. She moved through the spaces where the new arrivals moved and she paid attention and she let the Between do what it had always done since the experiments — reach slightly past what her eyes could see, feel the shape of intentions the way a hand feels heat before touching flame.

What she felt was not evidence.

It was a texture.

Wrong in a way she could not articulate.

---

She tried once.

Not with Saya.

She found the man with the careful posture in the corridor outside the eastern hall and she stopped him and she asked, simply and directly, what he wanted here.

He was good. She gave him that.

His surprise was real-looking. His gratitude was real-looking. He spoke about his children, who were not with him, who he had lost in the facility. His grief was real-looking.

She watched his hands the whole time.

She let him go.

She had nothing. Just the texture. Just the Between pressing slightly against the inside of her chest in a way it only did when something was being hidden from her.

She had learned, a long time ago, not to fully trust that feeling.

She had also learned what it cost to ignore it.

---

She made the decision alone.

Not quickly. She sat with it for three days, which was longer than she usually sat with anything. She turned it over from every angle. She asked herself, carefully and honestly, whether she was seeing something real or whether she was seeing the shape of her own damage reflected back at her.

She didn't know.

That was the problem.

She had never not known before.

---

She chose certainty over caution.

It was the wrong framing, she would understand later. Certainty was not what she had. What she had was a calculation: the cost of being right and doing nothing against the cost of being wrong and acting anyway. She had run that calculation her entire life. It had always come out the same way.

It came out the same way now.

She was quiet about it.

Thorough.

She left no evidence because there had never been any evidence to leave. These were people who had survived by being careful. She was someone who had survived the same way.

Afterward, she came back to her quarters and she sat with the stillness for a long time.

She did not go to Saya.

She told herself it was because there was nothing yet to say.

She would not be certain, later, that was the only reason.
