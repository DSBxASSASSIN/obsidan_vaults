# Scene: The Weight of the Room

## Context

Occurs during the Vaelos rebuilding operation planning period — a formal meeting with high command.

Involves:

- [Kael](https://claude.ai/chat/Kael.md)
- [Saya](https://claude.ai/chat/Saya.md)
- [Sorin](https://claude.ai/chat/Sorin.md)
- High command

---

## Scene

The meeting had been running for forty minutes.

It was a practical meeting — rebuilding logistics, resource allocation, the specific questions of what Vaelos needed and in what order and from where. High command had prepared well. The documents were thorough. The questions being asked were the right questions.

Saya was answering them the way she always answered things — precisely, without hesitation, the full weight of her attention on whoever was speaking.

Sorin noticed it first.

Not a dramatic change. Something small. The way her eyes moved between speakers had a half-second delay that hadn't been there before. The way she paused before answering Aldren's third question — not long enough for anyone to remark on, just long enough for Sorin to clock it. And once, briefly, her gaze went somewhere past the table entirely, focused on nothing visible, before she pulled it back.

He looked at Kael.

---

Kael was sitting very still.

Not her usual stillness — the controlled presence of someone choosing to take up a specific amount of space. Something quieter than that. Her hands were flat on the table. Her eyes were open but her attention was not entirely in the room. She looked like something that had gone deep inside itself and left a very convincing surface behind.

Sorin leaned slightly toward her.

"Is she alright," he said. Barely voiced. Under the sound of Aldren explaining the eastern district infrastructure assessment.

Kael's eyes moved to him without her head turning.

"No," she said. Same volume. Same stillness.

"What's happening."

A pause. Kael looked at Saya — at the slight delay, the carefully managed distance in her eyes — and then back at the table.

"I'll tell you later," she said.

Sorin looked at Saya again. Then he looked at the room — the twelve people around the table, the documents, the maps of Vaelos spread across the surface, everyone present and focused and unaware.

He said nothing further.

He stayed where he was and watched.

---

Kael had felt it building since they sat down.

The meeting room was full of people who had complicated feelings about Vaelos — grief, anger, the particular texture of loyalty to a place that had been taken and was only now being reclaimed. All of it moving through the Between like weather, like current, like a river that had too much in it today.

For Kael it was manageable. She had spent years learning to let it move through rather than accumulate. She went still and let it pass and kept the surface functioning.

For Saya it was not manageable yet.

She could feel her sister across the table — feel the effort of it, the way Saya was holding the line by force rather than technique, bracing instead of flowing, which only made it worse. She had done the same thing herself, early on. She knew what that kind of effort cost.

She needed to get Saya out of the room.

She looked at the maps on the table.

She looked at Aldren.

"The eastern district survey," she said, cutting across a question from one of the junior commanders in a tone that was not rude but left no room for continuation. "We're missing the updated water infrastructure assessment. We can't make allocation decisions without it."

Aldren looked at her.

"I have it here—"

"The updated one. From the on-site team." She held his gaze. "We should recess until it's confirmed. Twenty minutes."

A beat. Aldren looked at his documents, looked at her, and made the calculation that this was not a moment to push back on.

"Twenty minutes," he said.

---

People stood, stretched, moved toward the side table where someone had left refreshments. The room filled with the low sound of sidebar conversations.

Kael crossed to Saya before anyone else reached her.

She didn't say anything. She put her hand briefly on Saya's arm — not a grip, just contact, the specific warmth of a person who knew her — and tilted her head toward the door.

Saya stood.

They moved through the room without hurrying, Saya nodding to someone who caught her eye, both of them unhurried and purposeful in the way of people with somewhere to be. Sorin fell in behind them without being asked.

---

The corridor outside was empty.

Saya leaned against the wall the moment the door closed and pressed the back of her hand against her mouth and breathed.

"I know," Kael said.

"It's everyone in there." Saya's voice was controlled but only just. "All of them at once. Everything they feel about Vaelos just — arriving. I couldn't—"

"I know." Kael moved to stand in front of her. "Look at me."

Saya looked at her.

"Not at the wall. Not at the door. Here." Kael's voice was even, specific, the same tone she used when the frames went deep and she needed something to pull her back. "Just this. Just me."

Saya looked at her.

"Good." Kael held her gaze. "Now breathe. Not to manage it. Just breathe."

A moment. Then Saya breathed.

"The Between doesn't stop," Kael said. "You can't make it stop. What you can do is stop bracing against it. You're holding yourself rigid and it makes every wave worse. Let it move through. You're not trying to feel less — you're trying to stop being swamped by it."

"How."

"You need something to hold onto. Something that is only yours — only here, only present." Kael paused. "We'll work on that. But right now — my voice. That's the anchor. Just my voice."

Saya nodded once.

They stood in the corridor while the twenty minutes passed, Kael talking her through it in a low steady voice, Sorin at the end of the corridor with his back to them and his eyes on the approach, and gradually the rigid quality in Saya's shoulders eased.

Not gone. Better.

---

They went back in.

Saya finished the meeting.

It cost her, Kael could feel it — the sustained effort of someone managing something they didn't have full control of yet, holding the line for another hour by will and technique in roughly equal measure. But she held it. She was precise and decisive and nobody in that room saw anything they shouldn't have seen.

When the last document was signed and the last question answered and high command filed out, Saya sat very still in her chair for a moment.

Then she stood.

"My room," she said quietly.

Kael nodded.

Sorin followed without being asked, and neither of them told him not to.

---

### The Lesson

Saya's room was quiet.

She sat on the low couch near the window, legs tucked up the way she always sat when the formal posture was no longer required. Kael sat across from her. Sorin took a position near the wall — not the door this time, not outside, just present — and was still.

"Tell me how you do it," Saya said.

Kael looked at her hands for a moment.

"Badly, at first," she said. "For a long time, badly."

"You don't now."

"I have an anchor." She paused, finding the words for something she had never had to explain before. "Something that tells me where I end and the Between begins. When it gets loud — when there's too much moving through — I find the anchor and I hold onto it and it keeps me on this side of the membrane."

Saya was quiet for a moment.

"The gold," she said.

Kael looked at her.

"I never knew what it meant," Saya said. "But I always knew it meant something." A pause. "It's your anchor."

"Yes."

"What does it say."

Kael was quiet for a moment.

"That I am loved," she said. "That I have somewhere to go back to."

The room held that for a moment.

"I need something different," Saya said. Not a criticism. Just the truth of it. "Something that's mine."

"Yes." Kael reached to her belt. She unclipped something small — a dagger, short-bladed, worn smooth from years of handling, the kind of thing that had been carried so long it had become part of the person carrying it — and held it out.

Saya looked at it.

She looked at Kael.

She took it.

"Something physical," Kael said. "Something you can reach for without anyone knowing why. The sensation of it — the weight, the temperature, the specific feel of it in your hand — pulls you back into your body. Into the present. Out of the Between." A pause. "You practice with it until reaching for it is the same as being here."

Saya turned the dagger in her hands. Feeling the weight of it. The worn handle. The specific temperature of the metal.

She looked at it for a long moment.

Then she looked at Kael and nodded once, and clipped it to her own belt.

---

Sorin had been quiet through all of it.

He spoke now, from his place near the wall.

"Is it the same thing you do," he said. Looking at Kael. "In meetings. When you go still like that."

"Yes."

He nodded slowly. Processing.

"At Vaelos," he said. "In the throne room." He looked at Saya. "You said my father's name."

Saya met his gaze.

"Yes," she said.

"The files were corrupted. I checked twice." He was quiet for a moment. "You heard it. The way Kael hears things."

"I'm still learning what I hear," Saya said. "And what I don't."

He looked at her for a moment longer. Then at Kael. Then back at the middle distance, where people look when they are placing pieces into a pattern they have been working on for a long time.

"Aldric," he said quietly. To himself, mostly. Not a question.

Neither of them responded.

He didn't need them to.

---

After a while he looked up.

"What can I do," he said. "When it happens. When either of you are — when it's too much." He looked between them. "What can I do."

Kael looked at him.

She thought about every dark room, every corridor, every cup of something hot set down beside her hand without comment. She thought about the question he had asked her through a door — _can you go back now_ — and how much that single question had done.

"What you already do," she said.

He looked at her.

"You stay," she said. "You don't ask too many questions. You don't try to fix it. You stay and you make it clear you're there and you let us find our way back." A pause. "You've been doing it for years. You're already good at it."

Something moved across his expression.

He looked at Saya.

"For you," he said. "Is there something specific. That helps."

Saya considered this seriously, the way she considered everything.

"Talk to me," she said finally. "Not about what's happening. Just — talk. Something ordinary. Something that has nothing to do with the Between or the frames or any of it." She paused. "It gives me something to follow back."

He nodded once.

Filed it.

The way he filed everything — quietly, completely, without making anything of it.

---

The evening settled around them.

Nobody moved to leave immediately. The room held the three of them in the particular quiet of people who have just shared something that changed the shape of the space between them — not dramatically, not with ceremony, just the slow irreversible shift of more being known than was known before.

After a while Saya reached down and touched the dagger at her belt.

Just briefly. Just her fingers finding it.

Then she looked out the window at the dark.

## Notes

- Sorin notices Saya first — the half-second delay, the eyes going somewhere past the table
- Kael is managing it herself but in her own way — the deep stillness, attention elsewhere
- Kael manufactures the recess — practical cover, no drama
- The corridor grounding: Kael's voice as temporary anchor until Saya has something of her own
- The dagger is Kael's — worn, carried for years, given without explanation. The reader knows it's a dagger. The reader knows Saya was stabbed by Kael. The connection is not stated.
- Sorin's three questions in order: is it the same thing you do / Vaelos and the name / what can I do
- "What you already do" — Kael telling him his instincts have been right all along
- Saya's anchor request: talk to me about something ordinary. Give me something to follow back.
- The lesson is also Kael's way of telling Sorin about it later — she doesn't explain it to him directly, she brings him into the room and lets him listen
- The dagger at her belt at the end — Saya finding it without thinking about it. It's already working.
