# Scene: Uriel's Killing Spree

## Context

Occurs during an extended solo operation — several days of continuous fighting without rest.

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]
- [[Sorin]]
- [[Uriel]]

---

## Scene

She had not slept in four days.

She knew this the way she knew most things about her own body — not as complaint, not as concern, just as information. Four days. Seven bases. The intelligence had kept coming and she had kept moving and somewhere around the third day the Between had stopped feeling like something she was drawing on and started feeling like something she was standing in. Immersed. The membrane that was already thin had gone thinner still.

She had not noticed how thin until she opened Uriel's case.

---

The base was the largest of the seven.

She had worked through the outer sections methodically — the way she always worked, controlled, nothing wasted — and somewhere in the middle levels the methodology had stopped. She didn't register it stopping. One moment she was moving with purpose and the next the purpose had become something vast and luminous and total and she was inside it completely and there was no edge to find.

Uriel did not take her violently.

That was the thing she would not be able to explain afterward.

It was simply — complete. The connection went all the way down, past the part of her that knew her own name, past the part that knew what the gold meant, past everything she had ever used to find her way back. It found the bottom of her and kept going and there was nothing left to hold the line because the line had dissolved and she hadn't felt it go.

What remained moved through the base like light through water.

Thorough.

Radiant.

Utterly without Kael in it.

---

Saya came through the outer entrance with six guards at her back and stopped.

The corridor ahead of her was — wrong. Not destroyed the way a battle leaves destruction, the chaos of two forces meeting. This was something else. Systematic. Total. Every surface scored with the particular precision of something that had moved through here with complete certainty and no interruption.

She had seen Kael's work before.

This was not Kael's work.

This was something wearing Kael.

She told the guards to wait at the entrance. They looked at her. She told them again. They stayed.

She went in alone.

---

Corridor by corridor the evidence accumulated.

She did not let herself catalogue it too carefully. She kept moving. She followed the light — there was light, sourceless, emanating from somewhere deeper in the structure, the kind of radiance that had no business existing in a facility like this — and she moved toward it because Kael was inside it somewhere and that was the only thing that mattered.

She had done this before.

The Voruna incident had been the worst thing she had seen up to that point. Kael in Voruna, pushed past the edge, the frame's ferocity and her own compressed grief amplifying each other until something that was almost entirely not-Kael was standing in her sister's place.

Her voice had reached her then.

She kept moving.

---

She found her in the central chamber.

Uriel stood in the middle of a room that had been — remade, somehow, the Orokin frame's light rewriting the space around it, everything illuminated in that sourceless gold-white radiance. The frame's head was moving slowly, scanning. Looking for the next thing. Finding nothing and not having resolved what to do about the absence of it.

Saya stood in the doorway and looked at her sister.

"Kael."

Her voice was steady. She had practiced steady for most of her life.

Uriel turned.

The light shifted.

The frame's head oriented toward her — and then something happened that Saya had not expected and would not forget. The glow in the frame's eyes, that vast radiant intensity, _dimmed_. Just slightly. Just enough. Like something surfacing from very deep water, breaking the surface for one breath —

Kael's eyes.

For one moment, behind the light, Kael's eyes.

Saya breathed.

"Kael. Come back."

She took a step forward.

The eyes dimmed further —

And then the light came back.

Not gradually. All at once. The intensity doubling, tripling, something vast and luminous and completely without her sister in it locking onto her position — and Uriel moved.

Not a step.

A _dash_.

Saya had time to understand what was happening and no time to move away from it and she had always moved toward Kael, always, that was the thing she did, that was what she had always done —

The blade caught her across the stomach.

She went down.

---

The sound she made was small.

It was that sound — not the impact, not the fall, but that small involuntary sound of a body registering something it was not supposed to sustain — that did what nothing else had done.

Uriel stopped.

The light did not dim.

But the frame stopped.

And in the stillness something that had been buried under four days of depletion and the total weight of Uriel's connection began to _move_. Slow. Agonizing. Like fighting through water that had set into something thicker. The part of her that knew what the gold meant, that knew what Saya's voice sounded like, that had structured its entire existence around the fact that this specific person did not get hurt —

She heard the sound her sister had made.

She heard it.

And she _pulled_.

---

It was not clean.

It was not the way Transference was supposed to work — the smooth recession of connection, the frame releasing, the operator returning to themselves. It was tearing. She pulled and Uriel held not out of malice but out of sheer completion, the connection so total that separating from it felt like separating from something structural, and she pulled anyway, with everything four days of exhaustion had left her, with the sound her sister had made playing on a loop somewhere beneath conscious thought —

She fell out of Uriel and hit the ground and did not catch herself.

---

Uriel stood above her.

Still.

The light fading slowly now, the frame's head tilting downward toward where she lay. That angle — not threatening, not reaching. Something closer to incomprehension. The head of something vast and complete looking at the small broken thing on the floor that had been inside it and trying to understand what had gone wrong.

It had not meant to go wrong.

It did not know what wrong was.

It simply stood there, head angled down, the last of the radiance fading from its eyes, present and confused and entirely without malice.

---

Saya pulled herself across the floor.

Her hand found Kael's.

"I'm here," she said. Her voice was still steady. She had always been able to make her voice steady. "I'm here. I'm alright."

This was not entirely true.

Kael's eyes were open. Looking at the ceiling. Something in them that was trying very hard to process what her hands had done and could not find a place to put it.

"Saya—"

"I'm here."

"I heard you—" Her voice broke on it. "I heard you and I couldn't—"

"I know." Saya's hand tightened on hers. "I know. You came back."

"I hurt you."

"You came back." Firmly. The way she said things that were not negotiable. "That's what happened. You came back."

Kael closed her eyes.

Four days of fighting and the tearing disconnection and the weight of what she had just heard herself do settled over her all at once and she did not have anything left to hold any of it with.

She went under.

---

Saya sat with her sister's hand in hers and the wound in her stomach and did not call out immediately.

She allowed herself one moment.

Just one.

Then she called it in.

---

Sorin came through the door at a run and stopped.

Both of them on the floor. Kael unconscious, Saya upright by will alone, her hand still wrapped around her sister's. Uriel standing above them with its head bowed, the light entirely gone now, just a frame in a ruined room that did not know what it had done.

He took in the room in one sweep the way he took in everything — fast, thorough, without letting what he felt reach his face.

Then he crossed to them and went to work.

## Notes

- Kael had not slept in four days — the depletion is what lets Uriel take her so completely
- The connection is total and immediate — she doesn't feel it happen, there is simply a before and an after
- Uriel is not malicious — the frame's confusion afterward is genuine, the bowed head is the only thing it has for what it cannot understand
- The flicker — Kael surfaces for one moment when Saya calls her name, eyes dimming, recognition present — and then Uriel pulls her back deeper. This is what Kael carries afterward: she knew, and still couldn't stop it
- The sound Saya makes is what reaches her where nothing else could — not the voice, not the gold, not the Between. The specific sound of her sister being hurt.
- Saya tells her _you came back_ — not _I forgive you_, not _it's okay_. Specifically that she came back. Because that is the only true thing and the only thing Kael can hold onto.
- Kael passes out not knowing if Saya will be alright
- Sorin finds them both on the floor — he has spent years trying to reach them and now walks into a room where they are both down
- The chains go on Uriel after this. Not as punishment. As the only honest acknowledgment of what it is.
- Outside account: Uriel's killing spree. The frame went rogue. Kael is not in the official version of events.
- Inside account: the one time the anchor didn't work. The one time Saya moved toward her and it wasn't enough.
