# Scene: The Collection 

## Context

Occurs during the recovery period following [[The Locked Room]].

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]
- [[Sorin]]

---

## Scene

Kael opened the door to the collection room and stepped aside.

Saya walked in.

She stopped after three steps.

The room was larger than she had imagined — larger than it had any right to be in the relay's layout, though she understood now that Kael had had input on that particular architectural decision. The frames stood along the walls in their housings, each one maintained to a standard that had nothing to do with military necessity and everything to do with the specific care Kael gave things she considered her own.

And every single one of them turned toward the door.

Not fully. Not a step, not a reach. Just the heads — slow, unanimous, the way a field of something turns toward light. One after another until every frame in the room had its face angled toward Kael standing in the doorway.

Saya looked at them.

She looked at Kael.

"Does that happen every time?"

"Yes."

"Every time you walk in."

"Yes."

"Not for anyone else."

Kael moved past her into the room. "No."

Saya stood in the middle of the collection and turned slowly, looking at each frame in turn. Voruna with its wolf-pack geometry. Garuda with her bladed elegance. Valkyr's compressed fury visible even in stillness. Harrow's patient weight. The chains on Uriel, head still lowered. The others — a dozen frames, each one maintained, each one present, each one watching Kael move through the room with the quality of something that recognizes what it's looking at.

"They know you," Saya said.

"Some more than others." Kael stopped in front of Valkyr, checked something on the housing with the ease of long habit. "The connection doesn't fully close when I'm not in them. They remember."

Sorin had taken a position near the wall — inside the room, as had become his arrangement — and was doing what he did, which was be present without intruding and observe everything.

---

Saya walked the room slowly.

She asked about Garuda first — the bladed arms, the particular aesthetics of ferocity-as-art. Kael explained it the way she explained most things: directly, without softening it. What Garuda felt like from inside. What it asked of her. What the gold was for when she wore it.

Saya listened without flinching.

She asked about Harrow. Kael was quieter about that one. She explained the weight of it, the cost, the specific way it didn't let her forget what she was doing. Saya listened and did not push for more than Kael offered.

She asked about Voruna and Kael's whole manner changed — not softened exactly, but something that had more ease in it, the way people speak about things they have found their shape with.

"It recognized me," Kael said. "Before Transference. Before contact. It just — turned."

Saya looked at Voruna.

"Like they do now," she said.

"Like they do now."

---

They moved along the wall.

Jade — Kael explained it briefly, the ease of it, the frame that asked nothing. Saya looked at it for a moment and then looked at Kael and said nothing, which meant she understood.

Cyte-09 — Kael's explanation was precise and short. _Long range. I use it when the mission requires it._ Saya noted the particular flatness in her voice and filed it.

Wisp — _useful, strange, the Between resonates with it in ways I haven't fully mapped yet._

Mag — _precise. Clean. No excess._

---

Then Saya stopped.

She was looking at a frame near the end of the wall. Smaller than most. Elaborate. Draped in something that moved slightly even in still air, organic and curling and deeply, thoroughly whimsical. Beside its housing sat a small floating construct — rounded, smooth, built for someone to stand on, which made no tactical sense whatsoever.

Saya looked at it for a long moment.

"Kael," she said.

"I know."

"Is that a—"

"Yes."

"That's a surfboard."

Sorin looked at the frame. He looked at Kael. Something moved across his expression that he did not entirely contain.

"I would genuinely like to see that," he said.

Kael looked at him.

"I maintain it," she said. "That's all."

"You maintain a surfboard."

"I maintain the frame. The board comes with the frame."

Saya was still looking at Yareli with the expression she wore when she had found something she intended to understand fully.

"Why do you have it," she said.

"I have all of them."

"You pilot all of them."

A pause.

"I maintain all of them," Kael said.

Saya turned to look at her.

"You don't pilot this one."

The room was quiet for a moment.

Kael looked at Yareli. At the elaborate trailing details, the particular quality of joy built into the frame's design — not ferocity, not weight, not the deep resonance of something that had been through difficulty. Just movement. Lightness. The aesthetic of someone who had decided the world could be ridden rather than endured.

"I can't find myself in it," she said finally.

Saya waited.

"The frames I operate — even the difficult ones — I can find the thread. The thing inside them that I recognize because I have something like it in me." Kael looked at Yareli for a moment longer. "Some frames are too far from who I am now. Too much joy in them. Too much — ease." A pause. "I don't know how to be inside something like that anymore."

The room was quiet.

Sorin had gone still near the wall. His surfboard joke had run out of air somewhere in the middle of that and he had let it go without comment.

Saya looked at her sister.

She thought about a girl who had trained seriously and loved her future and sat in a receiving room reading and had a stuffed verak on her shelf. She thought about what it had cost that girl to become the person standing in this room. She thought about the particular grief of not being able to get back into something because who you were when you loved it no longer exists.

She didn't say any of this.

She just looked at her sister for a moment.

Then she moved on to the next frame and asked about it, and Kael answered, and the room settled back into something easier.

---

They stayed in the collection room for another hour.

Saya asked about every frame. Kael answered — some at length, some briefly, some with the particular economy of someone who had made peace with a thing and didn't need to explain the whole of it. The frames stood in their housings and kept their heads turned and the room held the three of them in the quiet of a space that was usually Kael's alone and was, tonight, something else.

Before they left Saya stopped one more time in front of Yareli.

She looked at it for a moment.

"She's been maintained," Saya said. "Carefully."

"Yes."

"You take care of things you can't use."

"I take care of all of them."

Saya looked at her sister.

"I know," she said.

She didn't say what she meant by it. She didn't need to. The room knew. The frames knew, in whatever way frames knew things. Kael looked at the wall for a moment and then looked away and that was the end of it.

---

## Notes

- The head-turning happens immediately when Kael enters — every frame, unanimous, only for her
- Saya asks about each frame seriously and Kael answers with appropriate weight for each one
- Sorin's surfboard joke lands and then doesn't — he reads the room and lets it go
- "I can't find myself in it" — the real answer. Too much joy. Too much ease. She doesn't know how to be inside something like that anymore.
- Saya doesn't push the grief of it — she redirects to Vahn's dog and pulls Kael sideways out of it
- The dog's name becomes a small argument that Sorin needs context for
- Yareli being carefully maintained says something Kael won't say directly — she keeps things she can't use. She takes care of all of them.
- Saya understanding what that means without it being stated.
