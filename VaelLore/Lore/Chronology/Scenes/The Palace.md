# Scene: The Palace

## Context

Occurs during the official return to [[Vaelos]] — reclamation assessment, approximately one week.

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]
- [[Sorin]]
- Aldren and a small working team

---

## Scene

### Day One — Arrival

Vaelos from the air looked like something that had been interrupted.

Not destroyed. Not entirely. The Orokin had taken it, not leveled it, and what they had taken they had preserved in the particular way of people who viewed everything as a potential resource. The city was still there. The structures were still there. The palace was still there.

From the air it almost looked like home.

None of them said this.

---

They came down in the outer district with a small team — Aldren and eight others, assessors and guard, people with work to do and the competence to do it. The plan had been arranged in advance. Aldren had been given authority over the practical work. The sisters were here to see what was left of the place they had come from, and the team understood, without being told directly, that this required a particular kind of discretion.

The outer city received them quietly.

People had come back — not many, but some, the ones who had nowhere else to go or who had decided that the place you were from was worth returning to even when it was difficult. Faces turned toward the party and held for a moment and then looked away. The work of ordinary life continuing around the fact of their empress walking through streets she had last seen burning.

Saya walked with her spine straight and her face composed and Kael walked beside her and neither of them spoke.

Sorin had asked about routes the day before. Which approaches were clear, which had collapsed sections, what the most direct path to the throne room currently was. The assessors had given him three options. He had memorized all of them.

The palace gates were open.

---

The first hiccup came in the entrance corridor.

A section of the ceiling had come down — not recently, years ago, cleared enough to pass but not repaired. Two assessors were already working on it, measuring and marking. A third looked up when Sorin approached.

"The direct route to the throne room is blocked from here," she said. Practical, neutral. Just information to the person who had asked for it yesterday. "The floor in the middle gallery gave way at some point. We haven't assessed the alternative route yet."

Sorin looked at her.

"How long."

"To assess? An hour, maybe two."

"Take the time you need."

He turned and found a place to stand near the wall.

Kael watched him find it. The controlled placement of a man making himself wait when he did not want to wait. She looked at the blocked corridor. She looked back at him. She said nothing.

---

They spent the first hour in the outer rooms.

The assessors moved through with instruments and notes and the focused energy of people with a job to do. The three of them moved through the same spaces with a different kind of focus — slower, the kind of attention given to things you are trying to remember you already know.

Saya stopped in a small receiving room off the main corridor.

Just stopped, in the doorway.

The room was intact — furniture still there, dust-covered, the arrangement undisturbed. A low table near the window. Two chairs. A shelf where something had been displayed and was no longer there.

"I used to come here to read," Saya said.

Kael looked at the chairs. She remembered Saya in one of them, unreachable without being absent.

"I know," she said.

They stood in the doorway a while longer.

Then they moved on.

---

The second hiccup came when Sorin could see the throne room doors.

Two hours of patience. The alternative route assessed, cleared, pronounced stable. They had moved through it — through the service corridor, the eastern wing, a route Sorin had not planned for and that took them past rooms he had not prepared for — and then they came around the final corner and the throne room doors were there.

Thirty feet away. Open.

One of Aldren's assessors appeared at Sorin's elbow.

"I need a moment. The eastern wing structural assessment — two sections that need a decision before we proceed."

Sorin looked at the throne room doors.

He looked at the assessor.

"Show me," he said.

Kael watched him turn away from the doors and follow the man back the way they had come. She watched the set of his shoulders. The particular quality of someone adding one more thing to a weight that was already considerable, without comment.

She looked at Saya.

Saya was already looking at her.

Neither of them moved toward the throne room without him.

They waited.

---

Twenty minutes.

When Sorin came back he looked at them still standing in the corridor and understood immediately, and something moved across his expression that did not fully pass before he composed it.

He nodded once.

They walked to the throne room together.

---

The doors were open.

The room had been treated as a record — debris cleared, the worst of it removed, otherwise left as found. The throne was still there. The high windows still caught the light the same way. The room was smaller than Kael remembered, the way all childhood spaces are smaller, but unmistakably itself.

Sorin stopped in the doorway.

Just a breath. Then he kept walking.

Kael and Saya stayed near the entrance.

This part of the room was his.

He saw his father from halfway across the floor.

He kept walking.

The armor was old pattern, pre-fall, royal guard. Intact. His father had fallen near the base of the throne steps — not retreating. Facing outward. The positioning of a man who had put himself between the throne and whatever was coming and had not moved.

Sorin crossed the last of the distance and crouched down.

He did not speak.

Kael felt the Between moving through the room — old grief, old violence, the resonance of something final. And through it, from Sorin, a name cycling without him knowing it was cycling.

_Aldric._

Over and over. The name of the man in front of him. Present the way breathing was present.

She looked at the floor.

---

Saya moved.

Quiet, slow, she crossed to stand a few steps behind him.

She looked at the armor. At the way the years had preserved him at his post.

"Aldric would be proud of you," she said.

The room went still.

Something changed in the line of Sorin's back — too small to be called a flinch, too present to be nothing. He stayed crouched. He stayed looking at his father.

"He would have been glad to know you kept going," Saya said. "That you found somewhere to stand."

A long moment.

Sorin reached out and put his hand on his father's shoulder — the armor, the place where his father's shoulder was beneath it — and held it there.

Then he stood.

He turned.

His face was composed. But his eyes had the quality of someone who has set something down that they have been carrying for a long time and do not yet know what to do with their hands.

"The files on my family," he said. "I was told they were corrupted."

"Some records take more work to find than others," Saya said.

A pause.

Sorin held her gaze a moment longer than the answer required. Then his eyes moved to Kael.

Kael was looking at the floor near her feet. She felt his gaze and looked up and gave him nothing — not confirmation, not denial. Just her face, steady.

He looked back at Saya.

"Thank you," he said.

Whatever else he had noticed he set aside. Filed. Allowed to wait.

Saya nodded once.

---

### The Middle Days

The palace gave things back slowly.

Not all at once. Room by room, the way memory works — a door opened and something inside it familiar before the mind caught up with why.

---

Kael found her training room on the second day.

The equipment was still there — old, dust-covered, some of it damaged. She stood in the doorway for a long time. She thought about being sixteen and certain and the arrogance of someone who believed they understood what they were training for.

She went inside.

She didn't think about it consciously. She just started moving — the old forms, the ones she had drilled in this room until they stopped being something she did and became something she was. Her body remembered them before her mind engaged. The dust came off the equipment when she hit it. Some of it held. Some of it didn't — old fastenings giving way, a support bracket cracking under force it had not been asked to bear in years, a training post going over entirely and taking part of the rack with it.

She kept going.

Not in anger. That was the thing — it wasn't anger. It was something more mechanical than that. Something that needed to move and had found a room full of things that could absorb it.

She became aware of Sorin in the doorway at some point. She didn't know how long he'd been there. She didn't stop.

Another piece of equipment gave way under her hands.

She looked at it on the floor.

"If I had trained harder," she said. Not to him specifically. Just — into the room, the way you say things when you've been holding them long enough that they need somewhere to go. "If I had been better. Back then. When it mattered." She looked at her hands. "Saya wouldn't have been hurt. Wouldn't have spent years being afraid of what I was becoming." A pause. "I stood in this room and I thought I was ready. I wasn't close."

The room held the silence.

Sorin did not move from the doorway.

He did not tell her she was wrong.

He did not tell her she was right.

He just stayed where he was — present, not intruding, the fixed point at the edge of the room — and let her have said it, because some things needed to be said somewhere and this was the room she had said it in and that was enough.

After a while Kael looked up at the damage around her.

She picked up the training post.

Set it back against the wall.

She stayed for another hour after that, quieter, and Sorin stayed in the doorway, and neither of them spoke again, and it was a different kind of quiet than it had been before.

---

On the second afternoon Kael found her old room.

She did not announce it. She slipped away from the main group while Aldren was walking Saya through the eastern wing assessment, took a corridor she remembered without meaning to, and stood in front of a door she had not stood in front of since she was nineteen.

She opened it.

The room was — hers. Still. Undisturbed in the particular way of spaces that had been left alone rather than emptied. Her things were still there. The arrangement was still the same. The window still faced the same direction, the same view of the inner courtyard she had looked at every morning for the first nineteen years of her life.

She moved through it slowly.

She did not touch most things. Just looked, the way she had looked at everything in the palace this week — learning again the shape of something she already knew.

She stopped at the shelf near the window.

Among the things on it — the practical things, the expected things — sat a small figure. Old. Worn soft at the ears and along the back where the fabric had been compressed by years of handling. A verak — a Vaelos pack predator, low-slung and broad-shouldered, made for running terrain that didn't forgive weakness. The toy version had been made with care once, its details accurate in the way of something crafted by someone who knew what a verak actually looked like.

She had no memory of not having it.

She looked at it.

She looked at it for a moment longer than she had looked at anything else in the room.

Then she turned away and moved to the door.

---

She did not see Sorin in the corridor.

She did not see him look past her into the room as she pulled the door closed behind her. She did not see his eyes find the shelf, find the figure, find what she had looked at a beat too long.

She was already walking back toward the sound of Aldren's voice.

---

Saya found the library on the third day.

Most of the books were gone — taken, destroyed, the shelves emptied in the systematic way of people who understood that knowledge was power. But the room was intact. The window seat was still there, the cushion gone but the alcove unmistakably itself.

She sat in it.

She stayed until the light changed.

---

They ate together in the evenings in a small room off the kitchen that the assessors had made functional — practical, unglamorous, nothing like the formal dining they were used to. Aldren's team came and went with reports and the ordinary noise of work. Sorin ate with them on the third night without being asked and nobody made anything of it and after that it was simply the arrangement.

On the fourth evening one of the younger assessors asked Saya what the palace had been like before.

Saya looked at him for a moment.

Then she told him.

Not as a record. Just — the way it had been. The light in the mornings. The sound of the city from the upper levels. The way the throne room felt on formal days versus ordinary ones. The garden on the west side.

The young assessor listened.

Kael listened.

Sorin, across the table, set down his cup and listened too, and did not look like a man doing his duty.

---

On the fifth day Saya found a small box sealed into a recess in the wall of one of the side chambers. Tucked away. Easy to miss if you did not know it was there.

She knew it was there.

She brought it to Kael without opening it.

Kael looked at it. Then at Saya.

"Mother's," Saya said.

"I know."

They sat down together on the floor of the corridor and opened it between them. What was inside was small and ordinary and completely irreplaceable. They stayed there for a long time.

Sorin positioned himself at the end of the corridor, faced the other direction, and gave them the time they needed.

---

On the sixth day they found the garden.

Overgrown, years without instruction, but there — on the west side, exactly where Saya had described it. Some of what had been planted was still alive. Some had become something else.

Saya walked through it slowly, touching things, naming some of them under her breath.

At one point she paused at a plant — barely recognizable — and said a name in old Vael.

"What is it?" Kael asked.

"I don't know what you'd call it in common tongue." Saya looked at it. "Mother grew it. She said it was stubborn. That it came back every year no matter what the winter did to it."

She looked at it a moment longer.

Then she kept walking.

---

On the sixth evening Kael noticed Saya pause at the entrance to the eastern corridor.

A half-step hesitation. Her head tilting slightly. Her eyes going to the far end of the corridor where it turned.

Nothing there. No sound. No movement.

Three seconds later one of Aldren's assessors came around the corner.

Saya continued walking.

Kael filed it.

---

### Day Seven — The Balcony

The last evening came in quietly.

The assessors had finished their primary work. The reports were done, the decisions made. Aldren had everything in hand. Tomorrow they would leave.

Tonight the palace was theirs.

---

Kael found the balcony the way she had found everything else this week — by following something in her chest that knew where it was before her feet did. The balcony off the upper royal wing, facing east, the view stretching across the city toward the horizon.

She had not been here since she was nineteen.

She stood in the doorway.

Then Saya appeared beside her and they went out together without discussing it.

---

The city was quiet below them. The sky was doing what it did at the end of the day here — the particular colors of a Vaelos dusk, the ones that did not exist anywhere else, the ones they had both grown up with and had not seen for years.

They sat on the low wall of the balcony — the way they had sat here as children, the way their mother had told Kael not to sit, the way she had done it anyway — and let the city be the city and the sky be the sky.

After a while Saya said: "I missed this color."

"I know."

"There isn't anywhere else that has it."

"No."

A long quiet.

"We'll come back," Saya said. Not a question.

"Yes," Kael said. "We will."

---

Behind them, inside the room, they heard Sorin take his post.

Not outside the balcony doors.

Inside.

The soft sound of him moving to the interior wall, positioning himself with the doors at his back and the room in front of him. The inside of the door. Not the outside of it.

Neither of them commented on it.

Neither of them needed to.

Kael looked at the horizon and felt him there behind her — the fixed point that had stood at the edge of every room she had ever occupied, now inside the room. Inside the moment. Given the view of the back of their heads that he had always been given of the outside of their doors.

The city spread out below them.

The sky held its colors a little longer.

The three of them held the quiet together — two sisters on a balcony they had grown up on, a man standing at a door he was finally on the right side of — and let the last evening be what it was.
