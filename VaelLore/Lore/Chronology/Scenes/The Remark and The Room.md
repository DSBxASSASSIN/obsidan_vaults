# Scene: The Remark and The Room

## Context

Occurs several months after [[The Recovery]]. Aldren notices something. Saya acts.

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]
- [[Sorin]]
- Aldren

---

## Scene

### The Remark

The meeting ran long.

Trade logistics, eastern district supply chains, the particular grinding work of rebuilding something that had been gone for years. Saya had been precise and patient for three hours and when it was finally done she stood and the room began to empty and Aldren fell into step beside her the way he sometimes did when he had something that wasn't quite a report.

"Empress."

"Aldren."

He was quiet for a moment — the brief pause of someone choosing how to say a thing.

"It's strange," he said. "Seeing them like that."

Saya looked at him.

"Kael and Sorin." He said it simply, the way he said most things. "They're back to how they were. Before — all of it. The exile, the mission, everything." A pause. "It feels uncanny. Like watching something run backward."

Saya said nothing.

"I know it's not my place," Aldren said. "I just thought you'd want to know. In case you hadn't noticed."

"I'd noticed," Saya said.

He nodded once and left her to it.

She stood in the corridor for a moment.

Then she went to find her sister.

---

### The Summons

She sent for Kael first. Told her it was a matter that needed her attention — private, her sitting room, this evening. Kael came without question.

She sent for Sorin an hour later. The same message. He arrived to find Kael already there, standing near the window, and something in his composure shifted almost imperceptibly before he settled it.

Kael looked at Saya.

Saya looked at both of them.

"Sit down," she said.

Neither of them sat. A beat — then Kael moved to the chair near the window and Sorin took a position near the wall, which was where he always stood, which was part of the problem.

"Not there," Saya said. "Here. Sit."

He sat.

---

"Aldren came to me today," Saya said. She did not soften it. "He said watching the two of you feels uncanny. Like something running backward." She looked between them. "He's right."

Neither of them spoke.

"I gave you months," Saya said. "I watched you be professional and correct and exactly what you're supposed to be and I waited for it to resolve itself." A pause. "It hasn't resolved itself. It's gotten worse."

Kael was looking at the wall.

Sorin was looking at his hands.

"I'm not angry," Saya said. "I'm done being patient. There's a difference." She sat back. "So. We're going to talk about it."

"There's nothing to—" Kael started.

"Kael."

Kael stopped.

"I had two conversations," Saya said. "Months ago. One with each of you. About what I saw between you and what I thought you should know." She looked at Sorin. "You were honest with me. I appreciated that." She looked at Kael. "You were resistant. I expected that."

She paused.

"What I did not expect," she said, "was for both of you to subsequently behave as though those conversations never happened and go back to being strangers."

"We're not strangers," Kael said. "We work together. We—"

"You used to finish each other's sentences," Saya said. "Do you remember that? Coming back from the mission. You didn't even notice you were doing it." A pause. "You don't do that anymore."

The room was quiet.

"Something happened," Saya said. "Between my conversations with you and now. Something that made it worse instead of better." She looked between them. "I don't know what it was. I'm asking."

Silence.

Kael looked at the wall.

Sorin looked at his hands.

Saya waited with the patience of someone who had already used up all her patience and had moved into something quieter and more absolute on the other side of it.

---

"The mission," Sorin said finally.

Kael looked at him.

"After the mission," he said. Carefully. Precisely. "When I was in medical." He paused. "I thought I heard Kael. Her voice. Saying something she wouldn't have said if she'd known I could hear it." He stopped. "I wasn't certain it was real — the surgery, the medication, the place between sleep and waking. I decided it was probably a dream."

The room went very still.

Saya looked at him.

Then she looked at Kael.

Kael had gone the particular quality of still that meant something had landed somewhere she hadn't expected.

"What did you think you heard," Saya said.

He looked at his hands for a moment.

"That you'd spoken to her," he said. "Named something for her that she didn't have language for." A pause. "That she said it probably wouldn't work. And that she was telling me anyway because she thought I was gone and she didn't want to have not said the true thing."

Saya said nothing.

She turned to look at Kael.

Kael was not looking at the wall anymore.

She was looking at Sorin.

"You were awake," she said. Very quietly.

He looked at her then.

"I wasn't certain," he said.

"You heard it."

"I didn't know if it was real."

"You heard it." Not an accusation. Just the fact of it, sitting in the room, taking up space.

He looked at her.

"Yes," he said.

---

The silence that followed had a different quality than the silences before it.

Saya did not fill it.

She sat with it and let them sit with it and watched her sister's face do something complicated and careful and Sorin's face do something that was composed on the surface and not composed at all underneath.

After a while Kael looked at her.

"You could have said something," she said. Not angry. Just — the specific flatness of someone recalibrating something they had built on a wrong assumption.

"I didn't know if I had a reason to," Sorin said.

"I told you—"

"You told someone you thought was unconscious." His voice was even. "That's different from telling me."

Kael looked at him for a long moment.

She did not have an answer for that.

---

Saya stood.

Both of them looked at her.

"I'm going to say what I said before," she said. "Both of it. I'm not sure this is a good idea. The hierarchy is real. What you both carry is real. The complications haven't gone away." She looked between them steadily. "And I want you to be happy. Both of you. And I have watched you be professionally miserable for months and I'm done with it."

She moved toward the door.

"I'm not deciding this for you," she said. "I'm putting you in the same room with the truth between you and leaving." She stopped at the door. "Don't go back to being strangers."

She left.

---

The room held them.

A long quiet passed.

Neither of them moved to fill it immediately. They had spent months being careful around each other and the carefulness didn't dissolve just because the reason for it had shifted.

Kael looked at her hands.

"I came every day," she said finally. "While you were in medical. When you were asleep." She didn't look up. "I learned your schedule. I came in the hours when I knew you wouldn't wake. I sat there and then I left before the watch changed."

He looked at her.

"You never knew," she said. "That was the point. If you didn't know then it wasn't — I didn't have to be anything about it. I could just be there."

A pause.

"I didn't know," he said.

"I know you didn't." She looked at the floor. "When you came back on duty I stopped. Because you could come to me then. And I didn't know what to do with that so I—" She stopped. "I converted it into duty. The way I do."

Sorin was quiet for a moment.

"I thought the dream was easier to carry than the alternative," he said. "If I asked and you said you never said it — that would have been worse than not knowing." A pause. "So I didn't ask."

"That's very—" She stopped. Almost something there, at the edge of it. "That's very like you."

"Is it."

"Carrying something quietly rather than risk losing it."

He looked at her.

"Yes," he said. "It is."

Another long quiet. Outside the room the relay went about its evening — distant footsteps, the low sound of the watch changing, the ordinary machinery of a place that didn't know something had shifted inside this particular room.

"I meant it," Kael said. "What I said. I'm not — I don't know what to do with it. I told you it probably wouldn't work and I still think that. But I meant it."

"I know," he said.

"I need you to know that I meant it. Not because I expect anything. Just—" She stopped. "I need you to know it was real."

He looked at her for a long moment.

"I know," he said again. Differently this time. Not an acknowledgment. Something that had more weight in it.

She looked at him.

For a moment neither of them spoke and neither of them looked away and the thing that had been filed and carried and almost-said and said-into-nothing sat between them plainly in the room where Saya had left it.

"I don't know what comes next," Kael said.

"Neither do I," Sorin said.

"That's — I don't usually operate without knowing what comes next."

"I know."

A small pause. Something in her expression shifted almost imperceptibly — not quite a smile, not quite relief. Something that had more ease in it than the room had held all evening.

"We'll figure it out," he said.

She looked at him.

Then she nodded once.

Once.

That was enough for tonight.

## Notes

- Aldren's remark is casual, practical — _it feels uncanny_ — not dramatic. Saya hooks on it immediately.
- Saya summons them separately, they arrive to find each other — Sorin's composure shifts almost imperceptibly
- Saya is done with patience, not angry — that distinction matters
- The accidental crack: Saya asks what happened, Sorin describes hearing Kael's voice, Kael realizes he was awake
- "You told someone you thought was unconscious. That's different from telling me." — Sorin's most honest line
- Kael tells him about the invisible visits — the only way she knew how to be present with something she had no language for
- "I need you to know it was real." — not a declaration, just the truth she needs him to have
- Saya's exit: both things at once, I'm not sure this is a good idea and I want you to be happy, don't go back to being strangers
- The ending: no resolution, no declaration, just two people who don't know what comes next and aren't running from it
- _That was enough for tonight_ — somewhere to start, not an arrival
