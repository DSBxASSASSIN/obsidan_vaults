# Scene: Repairing the Gold

## Context

Occurs after [[The Palace]] — back at Velanthas following the return to Vaelos.

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]

---

## Scene

The work was methodical.

It always was, with the frames. Kael had done this enough times that her hands knew the steps before her mind engaged — the cleaning first, then the assessment of what remained, then the careful laying of new trim over the places where the old had worn through or been stripped entirely. The gold went on in sections. Small ones. You couldn't rush it or the adhesion failed and you'd be doing it again in a week.

She didn't mind the slowness of it.

Voruna stood still under her hands the way it always stood still for her — not dormant, not absent, just patient. Allowing the attention. She worked along the collar first, replacing the section that had taken the worst of the exile, and let her mind settle into the rhythm of it.

Saya was on the couch to her left, legs tucked up, a datapad in her hands. She had been reading for the better part of an hour. The quiet between them was the comfortable kind — the kind that didn't require anything, that had existed between them since before either of them had language for it.

Kael moved to the shoulder section.

---

The memory came the way they sometimes came — not a flood, not a collapse. Just a wave. Present and then receding. She had learned to let them move through rather than brace against them.

She kept working.

Her hands on the gold trim. The familiar smell of the adhesive compound. The particular weight of Voruna's pauldron under her fingers.

And underneath the methodical surface of it — the thought that had been sitting at the edge of the work since she picked up the tools.

She had worn this gold into Garuda and Valkyr and Voruna and let things move through her that had no name for their hunger. She had stood in rooms afterward and looked at what remained and understood, clearly and without flinching, what she was capable of. The Orokin had cracked her open and this was what lived inside the crack — not just the deep connection to the Between, not just the Transference that came like breathing. This too. The thing that rose in the ferocity frames. The thing that needed the gold to remember it had somewhere to return to.

She was not so different from what they had made her.

She had never let herself think that thought to its end before.

She thought it now, hands steady on the gold, and felt it move through her the way the memory moved — present, and then receding, and then —

She glanced left.

---

Saya was not reading.

She was holding the datapad. She had not put it down. But she was not reading it — her eyes were somewhere past it, unfocused, and her face had done something Kael had not seen it do in a long time.

She was crying.

Quietly. The way Saya did most things — controlled, contained, not performing it. Just the fact of it, present on her face, tears she had not apparently chosen and had not stopped.

Kael set down the tool in her hand.

"Saya."

Saya blinked. Looked at her. Something moved across her expression — not embarrassment, something more complicated than that. The look of someone caught in the middle of something they don't yet have words for.

"I'm—" She stopped.

"What's wrong."

"I don't—" Saya looked at the datapad in her hands as if she had forgotten it was there. "I don't know. I was reading and then I just—" She stopped again.

Kael looked at her.

She looked at the gold trim in her hands.

She looked back at her sister's face — the tears still there, the expression still that complicated thing, the datapad still not put down.

Something clicked.

"Saya."

Saya looked at her.

"Can you hear me thinking."

The room went very still.

Saya's expression didn't change exactly. But something in it settled — the complicated thing resolving into something clearer and more difficult.

"Not — words," she said carefully. "Not always. More like—" She pressed her lips together. "Feeling. Pressure. Something that isn't mine arriving and I don't always know where it comes from." A pause. "I've been noticing it for a while."

"How long."

Saya looked at her hands.

"Since Uriel," she said quietly.

Kael was still for a moment.

Since Uriel. Since the stab wound, since the Transference collapse, since whatever the proximity to that much raw Void connection had done to the thin place in Saya that had always been there.

She had known something was changing. She had filed it, told herself it could wait, told herself they were recovering and some things were allowed to wait.

"Kael—"

"We should talk about that. What it means, whether it's—"

"Kael." Saya's voice was quiet and very precise. "What were you thinking about just now."

Kael stopped.

"The gold," she said.

"Before the gold."

A pause.

"Nothing."

"Kael." Not angry. Not accusing. Just her sister's voice, steady and inexorable, the voice she used when she had already decided that this was not a conversation Kael was going to redirect. "I felt it. I don't have words for all of it but I felt enough." Her eyes were still wet. "What were you thinking about."

Kael looked at Voruna.

At the gold trim she had been laying down. Section by section. The slow methodical work of restoring what the exile had worn away.

"The experiments," she said.

The word landed in the room and stayed there.

Saya did not speak immediately.

"Tell me," she said finally.

"Saya—"

"Tell me." Still quiet. Still precise. Still inexorable. "You have never told me. You came back from those rooms for months and you wrapped the burns before I could see them and you said nothing and I was sixteen and frightened and I let you say nothing because I didn't know how to ask." A breath. "I'm asking now."

Kael looked at the wall.

"It won't help you to know."

"That's not your decision."

"I've been making it for years."

"I know." Saya put the datapad down. Finally. Set it aside and turned to face her properly. "I know you have. Tell me anyway."

---

Kael told her.

Not everything. Not the specific texture of every session — some things she would not put into words, would not hand across the space between them, would not make Saya carry. But the shape of it. What the rooms were like. What they had wanted from her. What it had felt like to have someone reach inside the Between and pull, deliberately, with no regard for what the pulling did to the thing being pulled.

She said it plainly.

The way she said most things.

Saya listened without interrupting. Her face did not collapse — she was her mother's daughter, she had learned composure before she had learned most things — but something moved through it steadily, the way weather moves through a landscape. Present, and undeniable, and changing the shape of things.

When Kael stopped speaking the room was very quiet.

"They chose me," Saya said.

It was not a question.

Kael looked at the floor.

"They selected me," Saya said. "For the experiments. That was who they wanted." Still not a question. Her voice was doing something careful and very controlled. "And you volunteered to go instead."

"Yes."

The word was small in the room.

Saya was quiet for a long time.

"How long did you wait," she said finally, "before you told them yes."

Kael looked at her.

"I didn't wait," she said.

Something broke in Saya's expression.

Not collapsed — not Saya, she did not collapse, she had spent her entire adult life learning not to collapse because the people who needed her could not afford for her to collapse. But something broke anyway, under the composure, visible in the way she held very still and breathed very carefully and looked at her sister across the room.

"You didn't wait," she said.

"No."

"You just — said yes."

"Yes."

Saya looked at her hands.

She looked at them for a long time.

"All of it," she said quietly. "Every scar. Everything the Between did to you. The experiments and the frames and the gold and all of it." She stopped. "That was supposed to be me."

"It was never going to be you," Kael said. "That's why I went."

Saya looked up.

"That's not—" She stopped. Started again more carefully. "You don't get to decide that."

"I already did."

"Kael—"

"I would do it again." Simply. Plainly. The way she said things that were not negotiable. "I would do it every time. That is not something I need you to make peace with. It's just true."

Saya looked at her for a long moment.

Her eyes were wet again.

"I know," she said. "That's what makes it—" She stopped. Pressed her fingers briefly against her mouth. "That's what makes it the hardest thing you've ever told me."

---

She should have stopped there.

She knew, distantly, that she should stop there — that Saya had enough, that the shape of it was clear, that there was a mercy in not continuing. She had practiced that mercy for years. She was very good at it.

But Saya was looking at her with the expression she wore when she had already decided that a conversation was not finished, and the Between was still open between them, and there was no version of this where she looked her sister in the eyes and told her less than the truth.

So she told her the rest of it.

What the Orokin had understood about her connection to the Between — that the crack they had made in the membrane had also changed the nature of her relationship to death. The Between did not release people like her easily. She had learned this in the sessions. They had learned it too, and adjusted accordingly.

She had died in those rooms.

Not once. More than she had kept count of. The Orokin had ways of measuring how far they could push before the body stopped functioning, and they had discovered early that for her the answer was: further than it should be. The Between kept pulling her back. And so they had used that. Methodically. Without particular cruelty — that was the worst part, perhaps, how clinical it was, how they spoke about her in the third person while she was still inside her own body, as if her presence in the room was already hypothetical — they had simply used what was available to them.

She said it plainly.

The way she said most things.

---

Saya did not hold it together through that part.

It didn't come apart all at once. It came apart the way things came apart in Saya — in controlled increments, each one held until it couldn't be held anymore, composure giving way to composure giving way to the thing underneath all of it that had no composure left in it.

She was crying properly now. Not the quiet tears from before. Something that had more of her whole body in it — the grief of years of not knowing the full weight of what she was carrying, suddenly knowing.

"Saya—"

"Don't." Her voice came out fractured. "Don't tell me it's alright. Don't tell me you're fine. Don't—"

"I wasn't going to."

Saya looked at her.

Her face was doing something Kael had almost never seen it do. The composure her sister had built over a lifetime — the steadiness that had held through the fall of Vaelos and the Orokin's captivity and the weight of leading what remained of their people — was still there, underneath. But something had gotten through it. Something was moving across her face that belonged to the girl she had been before all of it, the sixteen year old who had watched her sister walk into a room and not understood what she was watching.

She understood now.

Kael set down the tool.

She crossed the small distance between them and sat beside her sister and put her arms around her and held on.

Not gently. Tightly. The way you hold someone when gentle isn't enough — when the only honest thing is the full weight of your presence, when words are inadequate and distance is worse and the only thing left is this.

Saya held on back.

She did not speak for a long time. Neither did Kael. The room held the silence around them — the frames, the tools, the gold half-repaired on Voruna's shoulder — and they stayed in it together while Saya's breathing gradually found its way back to something even.

It took a while.

Kael did not rush it.

She had nowhere to be. There was nothing more important than this room, this moment, her sister's weight against her and the truth finally between them after all the years of carrying it alone. She held on and let Saya take the time she needed and did not try to make it smaller than it was.

---

Eventually Saya's breathing steadied.

She didn't pull away immediately. Neither did Kael.

They sat together in the quiet for a while — not speaking, not needing to, just present in the room with everything that had been said and everything that didn't need to be said and the particular quality of silence that comes after something true has finally been spoken aloud.

After a while Saya reached up and pressed her hand briefly over Kael's where it rested on her shoulder.

Then she leaned back against the couch and looked at the ceiling and let out a long slow breath.

Kael looked at her.

Then she looked at Voruna.

At the gold trim, half-finished, the tools laid down where she had left them when she crossed the room. The section she had been working on still bare, still waiting.

She picked up the tool.

She went back to work.

Saya stayed where she was. She did not pick up the datapad. She sat in the quiet and watched her sister repair the gold, section by section, and neither of them spoke, and the room held them both, and the gold went on.
