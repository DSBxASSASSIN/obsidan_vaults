# Scene: The Attempt

## Context

Occurs on the day of the fall of Vaelos.

Involves:

- [[Sorin]]
- [[Kael]] — present but unreachable
- [[Saya]] — present but unreachable

---

## Scene

His father had been dead for an hour.

He knew this the way he knew everything that had happened in the last few hours — not processed, not landed, just present. A fact sitting beside all the other facts. The emperor and empress were dead. High command was gone — the first strike had been precise about that, architectural, the Orokin understanding that you take the head and the body follows. The city was burning in the districts closest to the palace. The Vael military was fractured and leaderless and moving on instinct rather than coordination.

He had watched the princesses being taken from a distance.

Not close enough to do anything.

Close enough to see which direction they went.

---

He found survivors where he could — the barracks, the eastern gate, the guard post near the secondary entrance where three men were still holding a position nobody had told them to abandon. He told them what he'd seen. He told them what he intended.

He told them he was asking, not ordering.

Fourteen men said yes.

He looked at them — some he'd trained beside for years, some he barely knew, some young enough that he'd have questioned their deployment under any other circumstance — and understood that this was what he had. This was the whole of it.

"Stay close," he said. "Stay quiet. We move together or we don't move."

He did not tell them what he thought their chances were.

They already knew.

---

The facility was Orokin — clean lines, controlled light, the particular aesthetic of people who believed that power was most itself when it was also beautiful. He had studied Orokin architecture in his training. He had studied it the way you study a threat — not to admire it, to understand how it worked and where it was vulnerable.

In theory.

In practice it was corridors and checkpoints and the specific cold of a place that had been built to contain things.

They moved fast. They moved as quietly as fourteen people can move in armor through unfamiliar ground. The first checkpoint fell before the alarm — two guards, quickly, nothing drawn out. Sorin noted what the facility felt like at that checkpoint. The weight of it. The size of what they were moving through.

He kept moving.

---

The first man went down at the second checkpoint.

Not from enemy fire. A door — pressure-sealed, triggered when the alarm finally went up — and he was on the wrong side of it. One moment beside Sorin, the next behind a wall of sealed metal that did not reopen.

Sorin looked at it for a moment.

Then he moved.

---

They lost two more at the junction on the third level.

A patrol response, faster than he'd accounted for. There was a fight — short and loud and nothing like the controlled entry he'd planned — and when it was over two of his people were down and the patrol was dealt with and the facility now knew exactly where they were.

He looked at the eleven who remained.

He did not speak.

He moved.

They followed.

---

It came apart in pieces after that.

Not all at once. Not a single catastrophic failure. Just corridor by corridor, checkpoint by checkpoint, the group becoming smaller in the way of things that are being worn down rather than broken — a man separated in a split route who didn't make it back, two more in the ambush on the fourth level that Sorin had known was coming and couldn't avoid, one who went down covering their retreat through a section they'd already cleared.

He kept count without meaning to.

He stopped counting at some point.

He kept moving.

---

He became aware of the silence somewhere on the fifth level.

Not silence — the facility had its own sounds, its own breath, the distant movement of the Orokin response working through the corridors toward them. But the sounds behind him had stopped. The particular quality of other people moving, breathing, making the small sounds of presence — that was gone.

He stopped.

He turned around.

The corridor behind him was empty.

He stood there for a long moment. Long enough to be certain. Long enough that there was no version of what he was seeing that had a different explanation.

Fourteen men.

He looked at the empty corridor.

Then he looked forward.

He knew the facility well enough by now to understand where he was. He had been tracking their position since entry — not on paper, in his head, the way he'd been trained. Fifth level. Eastern section. The holding areas were one level up, maybe two corridors further in.

He was close.

He was alone.

He was close.

---

He went forward.

Because his father had died at his post an hour ago.

Because he had seen them being taken.

Because the corridor behind him was empty and the one ahead was the only direction that still had a reason in it.

---

He found the holding level.

He found the section.

He got as far as the sealed door.

He put his hand against the metal.

Through it — faint, distorted by distance and wall and the particular way facilities like this were built to contain everything inside them — he heard her.

Not words. Not anything he could have reported or described with precision afterward. Just sound. The kind that a person makes when something is being done to them that should not be done, when the body has run out of ways to not make sound, when whatever is happening has been happening long enough that it has become the whole world.

He knew her voice.

He had catalogued it the way he catalogued everything about the people he had been trained to protect.

He stood at that door.

He could not open it.

He stood at that door for longer than was safe and his hand did not move from the metal and the sounds continued and he could not open it.

Then the response team found him.

---

They did not kill him.

He was not royalty. He was not valuable as a subject. He was a soldier who had made it further into the facility than he should have been able to, which made him a problem to be contained rather than eliminated.

They contained him.

He was processed, categorized, placed in a holding section in a different part of the same facility — far enough from the eastern wing that he could no longer hear anything through the walls. Close enough that he knew.

He sat in the dark and knew.

---

He was held for the duration.

He did not know how long. Long enough that the Orokin's interest in their captives ran its course. Long enough that when the empire finally fractured and the facility's systems failed and the doors opened in the chaos of a collapsing order, he had to reconstruct what month it was.

He walked out into the aftermath.

He found [[Kael]] in the wreckage.

He recognized immediately what the months had done to her — he had seen it before, in veterans, in people who had come back from things that should not be survived. He knew the shape of it. He knew what it meant.

He said nothing.

He presented himself as what he was — a surviving guard candidate, trained, Vael, with nowhere else to be.

She looked at him for a long moment.

He held still.

She accepted him.

He has been at his post since.

He has not told anyone about the mission.

He has not said the names of the fourteen men aloud.

He has not forgotten a single one.

---

## Notes

- Sorin is nineteen, the most senior surviving person by default — high command was killed in the first strike
- Fourteen volunteers. None came back except him.
- He gets close enough to hear something through the walls before he's captured
- He is held in the same facility for the duration of the experiments — separated, unable to reach them, aware in outline of what is happening
- He says nothing when he finds Kael afterward. He recognizes what was done to her and does not ask.
- The names of the fourteen are his to carry. He has never set them down.
