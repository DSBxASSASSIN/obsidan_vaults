# Scene: The Locked Room

## Context

Occurs immediately after [[Uriel's Killing Spree]].

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]
- [[Sorin]]

---

## Scene

She woke up in her room.

Not the relay. Not a field cot. Her room — the royal section of Velanthas, her own bed, the particular quality of light through her own windows. Someone had brought her home.

She lay still for a moment and let the ceiling be the ceiling and did not think about anything.

Then she thought about everything.

---

She did not move quickly when she got up. Her body had opinions about movement that she acknowledged and ignored and stood anyway. The room settled around her — her things, her frames along the wall, the familiar weight of the space she had built for herself in this place.

Uriel was there.

Someone had brought it back too. It stood in its place in the collection, head lowered, the chains she had not yet put on it hanging loose at its sides because she had not yet put them on it because she had been unconscious.

She looked at it for a moment.

It did not move. It had not moved. It was simply present the way it had always been present — contained, still, the light behind its eyes dim and quiet and offering nothing.

She crossed to the door.

It did not open.

She tried again. The mechanism was engaged from the outside — not locked, exactly, but held. The kind of hold that required a guard on the other side actively maintaining it.

She put her hand against the door.

"Sorin."

Silence.

"I know someone is out there."

A pause. Then, from the other side: a voice she didn't recognize. Junior guard. Young, by the sound of it.

"My lady. We — we were instructed to—"

"Where is Saya."

A longer pause.

"My lady—"

"Where. Is. Saya."

---

No one answered her directly for a long time.

She heard voices through the door — hushed, urgent, the sound of people making a decision they had not been prepared to make. She stood and listened to it and kept her hand flat against the door and did not speak again because she had asked her question and asking it again would not make the answer come faster.

She waited.

---

Sorin's voice came through the door eventually.

She exhaled once, slowly, at the sound of it.

"Tell me," she said.

A pause. She could feel him deciding how to say it. He did not dress things up — she had always trusted that about him — but he chose his words with the particular care of someone who understands that precision is its own form of kindness.

"She's alive," he said first.

Kael's hand pressed harder against the door.

"She's in intensive care. The wound was—" Another pause. "It required immediate attention. She's been in there since they brought you both back. The healers are with her. They have not come out to say it is going badly."

"But they haven't come out to say it's going well."

"No."

She stood with that.

"How long have I been unconscious."

"Fourteen hours."

Fourteen hours of Saya in intensive care and herself locked in her own room and no information between those two facts except what Sorin had just given her through a door.

"Why am I locked in."

Another pause. Longer this time.

"There was — a question of protocol," he said. "When you were both brought back. Both of you down at the same time, no prior arrangement for succession of command in that circumstance. The senior guards convened."

"They took a vote."

"Yes."

She almost — almost — found something to say about that. A decision made by a group of guards with no authority to make it, containing her in her own room while her sister fought for her life down the corridor. She could feel the shape of what she wanted to say about it.

She let it go.

"They were not wrong," she said instead.

A silence from the other side that she recognized as surprise.

"You were both down," she said. "No protocol. No clear next command. They did what they could with what they had." A pause. "Who was senior."

"Aldren. He — he wanted me to tell you he understands if—"

"Tell Aldren he made the right call."

Another silence.

"Yes, my lady."

---

She went back into the room.

She sat down on the floor with her back against the wall — not the bed, the floor, the specific grounding of stone beneath her — and she looked at Uriel across the room and she thought about what had happened.

She did not look away from it.

That was the thing she had always done with difficult true things — she looked at them directly, found their shape, understood them completely before she decided what to do about them. She had learned that the things you didn't look at directly had a way of becoming larger in the dark.

She looked at this one.

Four days without rest. The Between worn thin. The moment she had opened Uriel's case and the connection had gone all the way down without her feeling it go. The base. The corridors. She did not have clear memory of the corridors — fragments, the way dreams leave fragments, sensation without sequence. Light. Movement. The particular completeness of being entirely absent from herself.

And then.

Saya's voice.

The dimming — she remembered the dimming, the way something in her had heard her name in her sister's voice and tried to surface, tried to find the thread back. She remembered the trying. She remembered it not being enough. She remembered Uriel pulling her back down with the same indifferent totality as before, the recognition of Saya not a path home but simply a feature of the landscape Uriel was moving through.

And then the sound.

Small. Involuntary. The sound her sister made when the blade—

She pressed the back of her wrist against her mouth and held it there.

She had known Saya's voice since she was old enough to know anything. She had known it through the fall of Vaelos and the Orokin's facility and six weeks of exile and every dark place the frames had ever taken her. It had always been enough. It had always been the thread.

It had not been enough.

And the only thing that had reached her was the sound of her sister being hurt by her own hands.

She sat with that on the floor of her room and looked at Uriel across the space between them and did not look away.

---

Sorin came in once, in the late hours.

Not announced. She heard the door mechanism disengage and looked up and he was there, carrying two cups, moving with the particular quiet of someone who has learned to take up exactly as much space as is needed and no more.

He sat down against the wall beside her. Set one cup next to her hand. Did not speak.

She did not speak either for a while.

She looked at Uriel across the room.

The frame stood still. Head lowered. Quiet. It had not moved since they brought it back. It would not move unless she put herself inside it and she was never going to put herself inside it again and they both — in whatever way a frame could know something — understood that.

She thought about chains.

Not as punishment. Not as protocol. Just as the only honest thing left to do — the acknowledgment, in metal and weight, that this was a frame that could not be trusted with her. That she had found something real in it and it had nearly killed her sister and those two facts could both be true and the chains were what happened when they were.

"I'm going to chain it," she said. Not to Sorin. To herself, mostly. Working it out in words because some things needed to be said to exist properly. "Not now. When I'm ready. I'll do it myself."

He did not offer to do it for her.

She had not expected him to.

---

Another long quiet.

"Why fourteen?" she said.

He turned to look at her.

"When you're near me," she said. "The Between picks up residue. Emotion leaves a shape in it. Yours is usually contained — you're good at that. But there's a number that surfaces sometimes when you're close to me." She looked at the cup in her hands. "Fourteen. I've never asked what it meant."

The stillness that came over him was different from his usual stillness. That was professional, deliberate, the practiced quiet of someone who had decided long ago how much space to take up. This was something older. Something that had been carried so long it had become structural.

"I organized a rescue," he said. "When the Orokin took you." His voice was level. She had the impression he was choosing level very carefully. "After the fall. High command was gone. I found survivors — guard candidates, some military. People who were willing."

He stopped.

"Fourteen," she said quietly.

"Fourteen." He looked at the wall across from them. "I led them into the facility. We made it inside. Got far enough that I knew we were close." A pause that had a lot of weight in it. "By the time I reached the holding level I was alone."

The room held the silence around them.

"You reached it," she said.

"I reached the door." Something shifted in his jaw. "I could hear — I knew someone was on the other side of it. I knew it was you." He stopped again. "I couldn't open it. The response team found me before I could find a way through."

Kael looked at him for a long moment.

She thought about a nineteen year old standing at a sealed door in an Orokin facility with fourteen people behind him and none of them there anymore. She thought about what it meant that he had come out and they hadn't. She thought about the years since — the post he had never left, the way he had followed her into exile without being asked, the cup of something hot set beside her hand in every dark room she had ever sat in.

She understood now what it was he had been trying to finish.

"They followed you because you asked," she said. "You went because you couldn't not go." She looked at him steadily. "That is not a debt. Not to me. Not ever."

He was quiet for a long time.

"You can't tell me what to carry," he said finally.

She almost smiled at that.

"No," she said. "I suppose I can't."

They sat together in the dark and neither of them spoke again for a long time and eventually she slept and he stayed awake and kept watch the way he had always kept watch, and the night passed.

---

Saya woke on the second day.

The first thing she said, before the healers could stop her, before the guard at her door could announce anything, was her sister's name.

They told her Kael was in her room. They told her Kael was physically unharmed. They told her they would send word.

Saya looked at them with the particular patience of someone who was technically bedridden and had decided to be cooperative about it for exactly as long as it took to get what she wanted.

"Send her now," she said.

They hesitated in the way people hesitate when there is something they have not yet said.

"Her room is secured," one of the healers said carefully. "By guard order. Until you were—"

"Until I was what." Not a question. A very quiet statement that had the shape of a question.

"Awake. Empress. It was a precautionary—"

"You locked my sister in her room."

The healer had the good sense not to answer that directly. They sent for Sorin instead.

---

He came in and stood at the foot of her bed and she looked at him with the expression she reserved for situations where she was injured, bedridden, and still entirely capable of making a person feel the full weight of her attention.

"Explain," she said.

He did. Concisely, precisely, the way he did everything — both royals down simultaneously, no protocol, no clear line of succession, the decision made in the absence of any other option. Aldren's call. The vote.

Saya listened without interrupting.

"You voted," she said when he finished.

"Yes."

"You voted to lock Kael in her room."

"I voted to secure the situation until you were stable," he said. "Kael was the situation."

The room was quiet for a moment.

"I don't like it," Saya said.

"I know."

"She was locked in her room not knowing if I was alive."

"Yes." He held her gaze. "And if she had come out — in the state she was in, not knowing what Uriel had done to her, not knowing where the line was — and something had gone wrong—" He stopped. "I was not willing to risk that. Either of you."

Saya looked at him for a long moment.

She thought about what it meant that he had voted against Kael's freedom to protect Kael. The specific logic of it — keeping her contained not as punishment, not out of fear of what she was, but because he understood better than anyone what she would do to herself if she came out too early and it went wrong again.

She thought about every dark room and every door he had stood outside of.

"Fine," she said finally. The word carried more weight than its size suggested — not absolution exactly, but the acknowledgment that she understood, even if she did not like it. "But she comes to me now."

"Yes, Empress."

She held his gaze one moment longer.

"Thank you," she said. "For staying with her."

Something moved across his expression that he did not quite suppress in time.

Then he nodded once and went to open Kael's door.

---

The door to Kael's room disengaged.

Sorin appeared in the frame of it. He said nothing. He didn't need to. His expression said it plainly — _she's awake, she's asking for you_ — and Kael was already moving.

---

She stopped in the doorway of the intensive care room.

Saya was propped up against the pillows looking approximately as composed as someone could look after two days unconscious with a stab wound, which was to say not very composed at all and refusing to acknowledge it. She looked pale. She looked tired in a way that went deeper than sleep would fix. She looked — present. Entirely, completely present, her eyes finding Kael's the moment she appeared in the door with the same immediacy they had always found her.

Kael stood in the doorway and did not move because if she moved she would have to navigate the distance between them and she was not entirely certain what happened when she got there.

Saya looked at her for a moment.

Then she looked down at herself — at the bandaging visible above the edge of the blanket, the careful wrapping over the wound — and then back up at Kael with an expression that was doing something Kael did not immediately recognize.

"I've been thinking," Saya said, "that this scar is going to be very difficult to explain."

Kael said nothing.

"I'm considering telling people I got it doing something impressive." Saya's voice was mild. Conversational. As if she were discussing the weather in the outer districts. "Something that reflects well on me. I haven't decided what yet. I'm open to suggestions."

"Saya—"

"I'm also thinking," Saya continued, in the same tone, "that I'm finally starting to look more like my sister." She glanced down again. "You have several of these. I've always thought it was unfair that you had all the interesting scars and I had none."

Kael crossed the room.

She sat on the edge of the bed and she took her sister's hand and she looked at it for a long moment — the familiar shape of it, present and warm and real — and she did not say anything because there was nothing she could say that would hold the weight of what she needed to say.

Saya's hand tightened around hers.

"You came back," Saya said.

Quietly now. The lightness set aside. Just the fact of it, placed between them the way she had placed it on the floor of the ruined base — not negotiable, not qualified, simply true.

"You heard me and you came back."

Kael closed her eyes.

"I hurt you."

"You came back." The same tone she had used before. The tone that meant _this is the thing that matters and I will not let you replace it with something else._ "That's what happened. That's all that happened."

Kael did not argue.

She was very tired and her sister was alive and the hand in hers was warm and she did not have anything left to argue with.

She stayed.

---

Sorin stood outside the door and listened to the silence inside it and allowed himself, very quietly, to set something down that he had been carrying for a long time.

Not everything.

But some of it.

## Notes

- Kael wakes to find herself locked in her own room — she does not fight it, recognizes the guards made the right call
- The guards took a vote — no protocol existed for both royals being down simultaneously, Aldren made the call
- The conversation through the door is the first time Kael acknowledges to Sorin that she knows about the rescue attempt — she doesn't ask him to explain it, she tells him it isn't his debt to carry. He tells her she can't decide what he carries. Neither of them is wrong.
- Uriel stands unchained in the collection until Kael chains it herself — she will not let someone else do it
- Saya's first words upon waking are her sister's name
- The scar joke is Saya's way of saying _I chose you_ without making Kael carry the weight of a formal absolution
- "You came back" — the same words from the base, repeated. Not forgiveness. The only true thing.
- Sorin outside the door at the end — he tried to reach them once and couldn't. He's been reaching them ever since in every way available to him. This is as close as it gets to enough.
