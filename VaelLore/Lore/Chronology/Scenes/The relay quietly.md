# Scene: The Relay, Quietly

## Context

Occurs shortly after [[The Locked Room]], early recovery period.

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]
- [[Sorin]]

---

## Scene

It was Sorin's idea.

He presented it the way he presented most things — simply, directly, without softening it into a suggestion. He stood in the doorway of Kael's room on the fourth morning after the incident and said: "You both need to stop."

Kael looked at him.

"Not leave," he said. "Just — stop. Both of you. For a few days."

"Saya has—"

"Aldren has it. I checked." He did not move from the doorway. "The relay does not require either of you for three days. I have confirmed this. The only thing currently requiring either of you is the fact that you are both looking at reports you cannot act on yet and calling it recovery."

A silence.

"I know places," he said. "On the relay. Good ones." Something in his expression shifted — not quite a smile, the controlled version of one. "You helped build this place. You have never once seen most of it."

Kael looked at him for a long moment.

Then she looked at the report in her hands.

Then she set it down.

---

They went incognito.

Saya's idea, technically — she had appeared in Kael's doorway an hour later in clothes that were thoroughly not empress clothes, her hair down, the gold put away, looking approximately like a person rather than a ruler. She had looked at Kael with the expression she wore when she had already decided something and was waiting for Kael to catch up.

Kael had changed.

Sorin had led them out through the side corridor.

---

The relay was larger than either of them moved through on a normal day.

The parts they occupied — the royal section, the council chambers, the guard routes, the operational corridors — were a fraction of the whole. Sorin took them through the rest of it: the wide observation deck on the third level that caught the light differently at this hour, the small garden someone had planted in the interior courtyard that neither of them had known existed, the old communications room on the outer ring that had never been repurposed and still had its original fittings and a view across the dark toward the distant shape of Vaelos.

They did not go near that window for long.

---

There was a place on the lower level — a wide room, originally a civilian gathering space, half-converted and then left alone when the conversion ran out of purpose — where someone had put low furniture and soft lighting and it had become, apparently, the place where off-duty guards went when they wanted to exist without being guards for an hour.

Sorin brought them there in the late morning.

A few guards looked up when they came in. Registered them. Looked at Sorin. Looked back at their cups and their conversations and very deliberately continued existing without being guards for an hour.

Kael sat down carefully — not her usual controlled placement, just — sat, the way a person sits when they are tired and the furniture is available. She looked at the ceiling. She looked at her hands. She did not reach for anything to do.

Beside her Saya was doing the same thing, with the same quality of slight confusion, like two people who had been handed an instruction in a language they mostly spoke but hadn't used in a long time.

"I don't know what to do," Saya said quietly.

"Neither do I."

A pause.

"Is this what other people do?" Saya looked around the room — the guards talking, someone laughing at something across the space, the unhurried quality of people with nowhere to be for an hour. "Just — exist?"

"Apparently."

"It seems very—"

"Yes."

Saya considered this.

"I think I used to know how to do this," she said. "Before."

Kael thought about Vaelos. About being nineteen and having a future that was mapped out and certain. About the specific quality of days that had not yet become the shape her days were now.

"I think we both did," she said.

---

Sorin brought food at some point — not formally, not as service, just appeared with things and set them down and sat nearby in the particular way he had of being present without intruding. Kael ate without thinking about it, which she recognized distantly as a good sign. Saya ate slowly, favoring her left side without commenting on it, and Kael watched this from her peripheral vision and said nothing because they were supposed to be resting and she had promised herself she would not catalogue every wince.

She catalogued it anyway.

Old habits.

---

They found a corner of the observation deck in the afternoon where the angle of the light was good and the view was open and no one was likely to need them.

They sat in it for a long time without speaking.

This, Kael thought, was the thing she had forgotten was possible — the specific quality of silence with the one person whose silence had never required filling. She had spent six weeks in exile learning the weight of silence without Saya in it. This was the other kind. The kind that had always felt like the closest thing to rest she knew.

She let herself have it.

---

"Someone's coming," she said.

Not a warning. Just — the Between registering movement, the habit of noting it automatic after so many years.

And then she noticed Saya.

Her sister's eyes had moved to the corridor entrance before the words were fully out. Not toward the sound — there was no sound yet — but to the exact point where the corridor curved, where whoever was coming would appear. She was looking at it with the still, focused quality of someone listening to something just beneath the edge of perception.

The guard appeared around the corner a moment later.

Saya's eyes tracked him as he passed, then settled back to the view.

She said nothing.

Kael looked at her for a moment.

Saya was looking at the distance again, her expression easy, unhurried. Present. Nothing in her face that acknowledged what had just happened.

Kael looked back at the view.

She filed it.

She did not pull the thread.

They were supposed to be resting.

---

The evening found them in the interior courtyard — the garden Sorin had shown them, small and overgrown in the pleasant way of things that have been left to grow without instruction. Someone had put a bench in it that had no business being comfortable and was somehow comfortable anyway.

Saya sat with her legs tucked up and her head tipped back and her eyes half-closed, and she looked — young, Kael thought. Not the empress. Just her sister, the way she had looked before the fall, before the weight of everything they had become had settled into the lines of her.

Kael sat beside her and felt something ease that had been tightly held for a very long time.

"We should do this again," Saya said.

"We should."

"We won't."

"Probably not."

Saya made a small sound that was not quite a laugh. "We're very bad at this."

"Yes."

"Sorin is better at knowing what we need than we are."

Kael thought about the cup of something hot in every dark room. The fourteen men. The door he had stood outside of for years.

"Yes," she said. "He is."

Saya was quiet for a moment.

"I'm glad he's here," she said.

Kael looked at her sister in the garden light — pale still, moving carefully still, the wound healing in the slow way of things that had been serious — and felt the familiar ache of everything she carried on Saya's behalf and the specific new weight of what her own hands had done.

"So am I," she said.

---

On the last evening Sorin found them a spot on the outer walkway where you could see the stars properly.

He stood a little back from them at first, as he always did. Then, after a moment, he moved to the railing.

"I come here," he said. "On the difficult days." He was looking at the stars, not at either of them. "When I need to think about Vaelos. About before." A pause. "It's far enough from the royal section that nobody looks for me here. Close enough to the outer edge that you can see the distance." He was quiet for a moment. "It helps. Somehow."

Neither of them spoke immediately.

Kael looked at him — at the profile of him in the low light, the particular stillness of someone who has said a true thing and is waiting to see what happens to it.

He had never offered something like this unprompted. Not in all the years he had stood at the edge of her rooms and her life and her worst moments. He answered questions. He stayed when asked. He gave what was needed and kept the rest behind the work.

She did not know what had shifted tonight.

She thought perhaps it was simply the stars, and the dark, and the three of them finally standing still long enough that something had room to be said.

"I didn't know you came here," Saya said softly.

"There's no reason you would," he said. Not unkind. Just honest.

The stars held the silence for a moment.

Kael thought about the number fourteen. About a corridor he had never been able to finish walking. About what it meant that he was here, still, at the edge of this quiet moment, carrying what he carried and staying anyway.

She did not say any of this.

But she shifted slightly on the railing, making space beside her, and after a moment he moved to stand in it.

The three of them stood in the dark with the stars and the distant shape of Vaelos at the edge of the view and did not speak, and it was, in its own way, enough.

## Notes

- Sorin initiates the vacation — he has been watching them both not recover for four days and decides
- Neither of them knows how to do nothing — this is treated honestly, not sentimentally
- The Between hint: Kael says someone's coming, Saya's eyes go to the exact spot before anyone appears, she says nothing, Kael files it and does not pull the thread
- Saya is physically present but still in early recovery — she moves carefully, favors her side, does not comment on it
- The garden is something neither of them knew existed on their own relay — Sorin knows it because he has been standing at the edges of their lives for years
- Kael making space for Sorin on the railing at the end — small, unspoken, significant
- "We should do this again." "We won't." — they both know the shape of their lives too well for pretending otherwise
- The vacation does not fix anything. It gives them three days of being people instead of royals, which is its own kind of repair.
