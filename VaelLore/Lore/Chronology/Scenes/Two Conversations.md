# Scene: Two Conversations

## Context

Occurs during the recovery period — Kael and Sorin return from an extended mission together. Saya has not seen them for several weeks.

Involves:

- [[Kael]]
- [[Saya]]
- [[Sorin]]

---

## Scene

### The Return

They came back in the late afternoon.

Saya was in the outer corridor when she heard them — Kael's voice first, the particular focused quality of someone still in mission-mode, working through what had gone wrong with the eastern perimeter approach. Sorin's footsteps beside her, steady, the two of them moving in the unhurried way of people who had been in the same spaces for long enough that they didn't need to negotiate the distance between them.

She had not seen them together for several weeks.

She stood at the end of the corridor and watched them come toward her.

"—the second checkpoint was the problem," Kael was saying. "If we'd taken the lower approach from the start instead of—"

"Instead of trusting the survey data," Sorin said.

Kael kept walking. "Instead of trusting the survey data. Which was wrong by forty meters and put us directly into—"

"The patrol rotation."

"The patrol rotation."

Three seconds. Neither of them paused. Kael's sentence and Sorin's continuation and Kael's resumption — one thought, two people, no gap between them.

Neither of them noticed.

Saya noticed.

She kept her expression exactly as it was and stepped forward to greet them and said nothing about what she had just seen.

---

### The First Conversation — Sorin

She pulled him aside twenty minutes later.

Not formally — she passed him in the side corridor near the debriefing room and said, simply, "A moment," and he fell into step beside her without question.

She took them to a small room off the main corridor. Closed the door. Turned to face him.

Sorin stood with his usual composed attention. He had been debriefed, had handed off his report, had done everything duty required. He was present and unhurried and waiting to understand what this was.

"How long have you been finishing her sentences," Saya said.

A pause.

Something moved across his expression — not guilt, not quite. More the look of someone who has been shown something they genuinely hadn't seen.

"I don't—" He stopped. Considered. "I wasn't aware I was doing it."

"I know," Saya said. "That's what I'm telling you."

He was quiet for a moment.

"Saya—"

"I'm not reprimanding you." Her voice was even, direct, the voice she used when she had decided a conversation was going to happen and the only question was how long it took. "I'm telling you what I saw. And I'm asking you to be honest with me."

He looked at her.

"There's something between you," she said. "Something that wasn't there before, or wasn't visible before. And I want to know if you're aware of it."

A long pause.

Sorin looked at the wall. The particular quality of a man doing something he didn't do often — actually examining something he had filed and left unexamined.

"Before the fall," he said finally. "When I was in training. I—" He stopped. Started again more carefully. "She was the heir. I was a candidate. I never said anything. Never acted on it. She didn't know." A pause. "It was nothing. Just something I carried quietly and didn't examine too closely."

"But it was there," Saya said.

"Yes." He said it simply. "It was there. One-sided and appropriate to keep that way and I knew that."

Saya waited.

"I let it go after the fall," he said. "What came after was — there wasn't room for something like that. And I didn't feel I had the right to it. After the facility. After the fourteen." He said it plainly, the way he said most things that cost him. "I converted it into something else. Duty. Purpose. That held."

"And now," Saya said.

He was quiet for a long time.

"I don't know," he said. "I wasn't looking at it. You're making me look at it."

"I know," Saya said. "I'm doing it deliberately."

He looked at her.

"She's warming up to you," Saya said. "I don't know if she knows that's what it is. She probably doesn't — Kael doesn't examine things that aren't immediately relevant to the work. But I've been watching her for thirty years and I know what it looks like when she lets someone in." A pause. "I'm not telling you this to encourage it. I'm telling you because you deserve to know what you're standing next to."

Sorin said nothing.

"The hierarchy hasn't gone away," Saya said. "She's still who she is. You're still her guard. And you carry things that make it complicated in ways I can't resolve for you." She looked at him steadily. "But I'm not going to tell you what to do with it. That's yours."

A long silence.

"What do you think I should do," he said.

"I think," Saya said carefully, "that some things are better left as they are. That what you have with her is real and valuable and doesn't need to become something else to matter." She paused. "But I also think you're the only person in her life who has ever followed her into exile without being asked. And that is not nothing."

She let that sit.

"It's your choice," she said. "I just wanted you to be making it with your eyes open."

She left him there.

---

### Between

She carried what he had told her for the rest of the day.

The before-the-fall detail sat with her in a particular way. He had felt something, understood it was impossible, converted it into duty, and spent years building the most complete and particular loyalty she had ever seen from a guard — all of it growing in the soil of something he had decided didn't get to exist.

She thought about what things grow in soil like that.

She thought about her sister.

In the evening she went to Kael's room.

---

### The Second Conversation — Kael

Kael was at her workbench when Saya came in. Tools out, one of the smaller frames partially disassembled in front of her — the kind of methodical evening work she did when she needed her hands occupied and her mind quiet.

She looked up when Saya entered.

"You have your talking face on," she said.

"I always have this face."

"You have it more deliberately right now."

Saya sat down on the edge of the workbench and looked at her sister.

"I want to ask you something," she said. "And I want you to not immediately redirect it."

Kael set down the tool in her hand.

"Sorin," Saya said.

A pause.

"What about him."

"You finished each other's sentences today. Coming back from the mission. You didn't notice."

Kael looked at the frame in front of her. "We work together. We've been in the field together for—"

"Kael."

"—several weeks and it's natural that—"

"Kael." Saya's voice was patient and immovable. "I'm not asking about the field work."

Silence.

"I don't know what you're asking," Kael said.

"Yes you do."

"Saya—"

"What do you feel when he's in the room."

Kael picked up the tool again. Set it back down. Looked at the wall.

"He's my guard," she said. "I'm aware of him the way I'm aware of the guard. It's—"

"That's not what I asked."

"I know what you asked."

"Then answer it."

Kael was quiet for a long moment.

"I don't know how to answer it," she said finally. The particular flatness of someone saying something true that they would have preferred not to say. "I don't — I don't have a reference point for what you're pointing at. I know what it means to care about someone's survival. I know what it means to trust someone. I know what the gold is." She stopped. "But what you're describing — I don't have that. I don't know what that feels like. I've never had room to find out."

Saya looked at her sister.

She thought about nineteen years old and a fall and a facility and everything that came after. She thought about how much of Kael's interior life had been converted, like Sorin's, into something that could survive what happened to her. Duty. Purpose. The gold. The anchor.

"What does it feel like," Saya said gently, "when he sets something down beside you and doesn't ask anything."

Kael looked at her hands.

"Like I can breathe," she said. Very quietly.

"That's what I'm pointing at," Saya said.

The room was quiet.

"I don't know what to do with that," Kael said.

"I know." Saya looked at her steadily. "Kael. What you just described — that's what love feels like. The quiet kind. The kind that doesn't announce itself."

Kael looked at her.

"I'm not telling you what to do with it," Saya said. "I'm just telling you what it is. Because you'll spend the next year filing it under something else if I don't."

Kael looked at the disassembled frame in front of her for a long time.

"Does he—" She stopped.

"I'm not going to answer that," Saya said. "That's a conversation you'd have to have yourself."

Kael looked at her.

"I'm not telling you what to do," Saya said. "It's complicated and you know why it's complicated and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But you deserve to know what you're feeling before it gets decided for you by everything else." A pause. "That's all I'm saying."

She stood.

She crossed to the door.

She stopped there with her back to the room — the way she sometimes stopped, the habit of one more thing left at the threshold.

"He followed you into exile," she said. "Without being asked."

She left.

---

Kael sat at the workbench for a long time after.

She did not pick up the tool.

She sat with what her sister had said — the specific weight of it, the thing she had been filing without knowing she was filing it, suddenly sitting where she could see it.

_Like I can breathe._

She had said it before she thought about whether to say it.

She looked at the frame in front of her. At the tools. At the ordinary surface of an ordinary evening that had become something else entirely.

She did not know what to do with it.

She did not file it.

She let it sit.

---

## Notes

- The sentence-finishing is the tell — three seconds, neither of them notices, Saya notices everything
- Sorin's honesty: before the fall there was something, he understood it was impossible, converted it to duty after the facility and the fourteen because he didn't feel he had the right
- "You're the only person in her life who has ever followed her into exile without being asked" — Saya not telling him what to do but not pretending it means nothing either
- Kael's denial is rooted in not having a reference point — she doesn't know what romantic love feels like, has never had room for it
- "Like I can breathe" — said before she can stop herself, which is the most honest thing in the scene
- Saya doesn't tell either of them what the other said
- Both of them are left sitting with something dislodged, neither knowing the other is doing the same
- Saya leaves both conversations the same way — it's your choice, I'm not deciding for you
- Neither Kael nor Sorin does anything with it yet. They just carry it where they can see it.
