## Scene

The doors opened before the signal finished transmitting.

They always did. No one made Kael wait.

She stepped through the threshold without slowing, and the guards closest to the entrance registered two things in quick succession: the Warframe was still active, which it shouldn't have been, and the blood covering it wasn't fresh. Not dripping. Set into the frame in layers, dried into the grooves of it, too much to belong to a single fight.

"Stand down," one of them said, too quickly and too close.

Kael didn't respond. She didn't turn. She kept walking — each step measured, precise, controlled.

Almost.

---

Inside her quarters, the door sealed behind her and the silence that followed was the kind that felt like it was waiting for something.

Then the Warframe shifted. Not outward — inward, a tightening, like something pulling against itself. Kael reached for the disconnect.

It didn't take.

That was new. She tried again. The connection held, not resisting and not releasing, simply _holding_, and her breathing changed in response — shorter, less controlled — and the Warframe mirrored it. Small movements at first: a flex of the fingers, a tilt of the head, micro-adjustments that didn't resolve into stillness. The room felt too small.

She stepped back.

The Warframe stepped harder. The force cracked the flooring beneath it.

Kael stilled. Forced stillness, held with everything she had. For a second it worked.

Then something slipped.

---

The first strike wasn't intentional — a hand against the wall, too much force, metal bending and then breaking, the sound carrying through the door. Outside, the guards shifted. Inside, Kael didn't move again immediately, and that was the worst part. The pause. The space where control should have returned and didn't.

When the next movement came it was faster, less contained. The Warframe turned sharply and struck again, then again — controlled violence without direction, without target, without the thing that usually made violence purposeful.

Not rage. Not loss.

**Overflow.**

---

The door opened. They shouldn't have opened it, but they did — three guards, weapons raised, not firing. Not yet.

"Kael—"

They didn't finish. The Warframe moved, not toward them but _through_ them, a lunge that stopped just short and corrected mid-motion with a speed and precision that was somehow worse than if it had connected. One of them stumbled back. Another didn't move at all — not frozen, but choosing, trained and understanding what this was even as the fear moved through them anyway.

"Stand down," one of them tried again.

The Warframe tilted its head, just slightly, as if considering the instruction and finding it interesting. Then it stepped forward — not attacking, not stopping, simply closing distance in the particular way that makes people break. Two of them did. One held. Barely.

---

"Kael."

The voice cut through everything. Not louder than the others. Not sharper. Just — clear, in the way that only one voice had ever been clear to her.

The Warframe stopped completely.

Saya stood in the doorway without a weapon and without hesitation. She stepped inside, past the guards, and they didn't try to stop her.

---

Up close, the damage was worse — not to the Warframe itself but to the space around it, to the way it held itself. Too tight. Too ready. Like something that had forgotten how to release tension because it couldn't remember what release felt like.

Saya didn't rush. Didn't reach. Didn't raise her voice.

"It's me."

A pause, long enough to matter. The Warframe shifted — barely, just a fracture in the posture, a millimeter of something loosening — and Saya stepped closer, close enough that anyone else in the room would have pulled her back. No one did. No one moved.

"You can come back."

The same words. The same tone. No variation, no doubt, no question buried inside the sentence.

---

Inside the connection, something loosened. Not fully, not cleanly, but enough that Kael could feel the edge of herself again — still there, still reachable — and she pulled toward it harder than she had before. Not forcing the Warframe. Pulling herself out of it.

The disconnect hit all at once. No transition, no easing. One moment contained, the next —

Gone.

---

Kael collapsed before she hit the ground. Saya caught her, not cleanly, but enough. Behind them the Warframe stood empty again, or close enough to it that the difference didn't matter right now.

Silence returned slowly. The guards didn't move, didn't speak, didn't lower their weapons until Saya did.

---

Kael didn't wake for a long time. When she did, it wasn't confusion that settled first.

It was understanding.

---

Later, alone, she looked at the Warframe. At what it had become with her inside it. At what she had almost not come back from. She stayed with that for a while — the specific shape of almost, the distance between where she had been and where she had ended up, the thing that had bridged it.

That was when the idea formed. Not fully. Not as a system or a practice or a rule.

Just a thought. Clear, simple, necessary.

Next time, she would not go in empty.
