## Scene

Saya found out on a Tuesday.

She came to Kael directly, which meant she already knew. If she had been uncertain she would have sent someone first.

She was not uncertain.

---

Kael was in the eastern courtyard when she heard the footsteps. She didn't turn immediately. She finished what she was doing — a slow adjustment to the bracer on her left arm, a small thing, a nothing thing — and then she turned.

Saya's face was very still.

That was worse than anger would have been.

---

"Tell me it isn't true."

Kael said nothing.

"Tell me there's something I'm missing." Saya's voice was controlled. Careful in the way of someone who has decided in advance to be careful because they know what happens if they aren't. "Tell me you had a reason. Tell me anything."

Kael looked at her.

She had reasons. She had the texture of the Between pressing against her ribs for three weeks. She had the way the man's hands had stayed too still when she asked him about his children. She had the specific quality of attention the woman near the back had fixed on Saya's throat when she wasn't watching.

She had no evidence.

She said nothing.

---

Something shifted in Saya's expression.

Not breaking. Closing.

"I stood in front of our people," Saya said, "and I told them those survivors were safe. I spent weeks on that recovery. I called in obligations I hadn't finished rebuilding." A pause. "I told them we were still us. That we still valued our own."

"Saya—"

"Don't." The word was quiet and absolute. "If you had something to tell me, you would have told me before."

That landed.

Because it was true.

---

The silence that followed was the kind that changes things permanently.

Kael had stood in a lot of silences. She knew the difference between the ones that passed and the ones that didn't. She felt this one settling around them like something hardening, and she did not reach for words to stop it, because she had no words that would help and she knew the difference between those too.

"I've been watching you," Saya said finally.

Kael waited.

"Since the Gold Anchor failure. Since the Warframe." Something moved across Saya's face — not cruelty, nothing like cruelty, something much more painful than that. "I've been watching you and telling myself I was wrong. That it was just the aftermath. That you were still—"

She stopped.

Started again differently.

"I don't know what you are right now. I don't know if what happened in those rooms with the Orokin finally—" She pressed her lips together. "I don't know. And you're not telling me. You have never told me. And eleven of our people are dead because of a decision you made alone and silently and I cannot—"

Her voice didn't break.

But it came close.

"I cannot have you here right now."

---

It was formal.

It was witnessed — two of Saya's advisors stood at the far end of the courtyard, close enough to hear, and Kael understood that this was not an accident. Saya was an empress. She knew what formal meant and what informal meant and she had chosen this deliberately, which meant the return would also have to be formal, which meant it would have to be earned in a way that other people could see.

Kael understood all of this.

She nodded once.

"How long," she said.

"I don't know."

That was honest, at least.

---

She left before the day was out.

She didn't pack much. She never packed much. She took the Warframes she'd need and the few things that mattered and she walked out through the same gate the splinter group had come through and she didn't look back.

Not because it didn't hurt.

Because looking back wouldn't change anything, and she had always understood that about herself.

---

Sorin was at the gate when she came through it.

He wasn't supposed to be. He'd been on rotation elsewhere and there was no reason for him to be standing exactly there at exactly that hour unless he'd heard and positioned himself deliberately, which she knew immediately was what had happened.

He didn't say anything.

Neither did she.

She walked past him and he fell into step two paces behind her — the old distance, the trained distance, the one that meant _I am here and not in your way_ — and she let him walk with her until the gate was out of sight.

Then she stopped.

"Go back," she said.

"No."

She looked at him.

He looked back.

"That's an order."

"Yes," he said. And didn't move.

She held his gaze for a moment. Then she kept walking.

He followed.

She didn't tell him where she was going. He didn't ask.

---

She was halfway across the outer district when she stopped.

The gold trim caught the light.

She looked down at it for a moment. The subtle accent along the edge of her coat — present the way it was always present, quiet, not announcing itself.

Still intact.

Still saying what it had always said.

She stood with that for a moment — the specific weight of carrying a thing that said _I am loved, I have a place at her side_ while walking away from both.

Then she kept walking.

---

## Notes

- Saya's formal choice is deliberate — she is acting as empress, not just as a sister
- Kael does not defend herself because she has nothing that would hold
- "What if she's right" — the doubt turns inward for the first time
- The gold is intact when she leaves — it will not stay that way
