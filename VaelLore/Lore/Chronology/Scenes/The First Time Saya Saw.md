# Scene: The First Time Saya Saw

## Scene

Saya heard it before she saw it.

Not a single sound.

A sequence.

Metal shifting.

Something heavy dragging, then stopping, then moving again.

Not uncontrolled.

Measured.

That was what unsettled her.

She stepped into the corridor slowly.

The lights were still failing here—long stretches of shadow broken by thin bands of gold.

For a moment, she saw nothing.

Then the shape resolved.

At the far end of the hall.

Standing still.

Covered in blood.

Not splattered.

Layered.

Deliberate.

The Warframe did not move.

But it was not empty.

She could feel it.

The same way she had always felt Kael standing too close beside her.

Familiar.

And wrong in a way she didn’t have a name for.

Saya didn’t call out immediately.

She took one step forward.

Then another.

The Warframe moved.

Not toward her.

A slight shift of weight.

A turn.

Not fast.

Not violent.

Just enough to face her.

Saya stopped.

Her pulse had picked up, but her voice didn’t follow it.

“Kael.”

The name settled into the space between them.

The Warframe froze completely.

For a moment, nothing changed.

Then—

Slowly—

It lowered its head.

Not fully.

Not submission.

Recognition.

Saya let out a breath she hadn’t realized she was holding.

She stepped closer.

There was something in the way it held itself.

Tension that hadn’t resolved.

Like something still waiting for a command that wasn’t coming.

“You can come back,” she said quietly.

No response.

Not in words.

But something shifted.

Subtle.

Internal.

The pressure in the room eased just slightly.

Saya didn’t reach for it.

Didn’t try to touch.

She stayed where she was.

“I’m here.”

Another pause.

Longer this time.

Then the Warframe moved again.

Not outward.

Inward.

A controlled stillness replacing something sharper.

Something that had been close to breaking.

Saya nodded once.

Not because it was over.

Because it wasn’t.

But because—

That was enough.

She didn’t ask what had happened.

Didn’t ask what it felt like.

Didn’t ask what it had done.

She only said:

“Come on.”

And turned.

Not checking if it would follow.

She already knew it would.
