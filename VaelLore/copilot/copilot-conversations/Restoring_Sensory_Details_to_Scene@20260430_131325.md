## Scene

She found her in an abandoned relay station three districts out, a skeletal structure of rusted alloy and flickering emergency lights that smelled of ozone and decades of dust.

Kael heard the transport long before it breached the local airspace. The low-frequency thrum of the engines vibrated through the floor plating, rattling the loose components on the desk beside her. She didn't move. She remained sitting on the cold floor of what had once been a high-priority communications hub, her back pressed against a wall covered in peeling insulation. 

In front of her, a half-assembled evidence file was spread across the surface like a map of a war zone. She tilted her head back, watching the ceiling tiles shiver as she recognized the specific, rhythmic engine signature of the royal fleet.

Then, with a slow exhale that puffed in the chilled air, she looked back at the file.

---

The transport settled with a heavy, pressurized hiss that echoed through the empty corridors of the station.

Nothing happened for a long time. Kael sat in the returning silence, counting the seconds by the cooling click of the transport’s hull outside. She noted the lack of heavy footfalls, the absence of a tactical squad. She noted what it meant that Saya had come alone, had tracked her to this ghost of a building, and was now standing just beyond the threshold.

Waiting. 

Making sure of the air she was about to breathe.

Kael didn't rush her. She let her sister take the time she needed to find her footing in a place that felt like the end of the world.

---

Saya came through the door without a word, her silhouette cutting through the haze of dust motes dancing in the emergency strobes.

Sorin was waiting just outside the frame of the door. He stood at his post with a terrifying, formal precision—the posture of a man who had decided that if he was going to be a traitor, he would at least be the most disciplined one in the history of the guard.

Saya stopped when she saw him, her gaze raking over his travel-worn uniform.

"You know she's exiled," she said, her voice echoing flatly against the metal walls.

"Yes."

"Which makes you AWOL."

"Yes."

She held his gaze, searching for a flicker of doubt. He held hers with the steady, unblinking resolve of a man who had already made his peace with the consequences.

"I'm doing my duty," Sorin said, his voice low and resonant. "I'm doing my duty. Guarding royalty."

Something flickered across Saya's expression—a brief, painful softening—before she stepped past him and into the room.

---

Her eyes didn't go to Kael first. They moved to **Voruna**, propped against the far wall.

It was the only thing Kael had taken—the singular frame she had carried into the dark. Saya stared at it, taking in the brutal geography of the last six weeks. The armor was a map of violence: deep scoring across the chest plate, edges worn down to the raw substrate, and patches where the finish had been stripped away by atmospheric friction or close-quarters fire.

Then her eyes moved to the gold trim.

The regal filigree was a ruin. Large sections were missing entirely, leaving jagged gaps in the design. What remained was scored and dull, the luster replaced by a matte degradation that looked less like battle damage and more like a slow, systemic rot.

---

"You left evidence," Saya said, finally turning to her sister.

"Yes."

"You didn't come back with it."

"No."

A heavy pause followed. Saya looked down at the papers and data drives fanned out across the floor. "You're still building it."

"It's not airtight yet," Kael replied, her voice sounding hollow in the vast room.

"It doesn't need to be airtight. I saw what you left in the plaza. I understand what it means." Saya’s voice was steady, but there was an edge of desperation beneath it. "I need you to tell me why you didn't come back."

"You exiled me, Saya."

"I need to know why you didn't come back when you had something to bring me. Why you're still out here in the dirt."

---

Kael looked at the file, her fingers tracing the edge of a thermal scan. Then she looked up, her eyes shadowed.

"Because I needed to be sure."

"Of the evidence?"

"Of myself."

The silence that followed was absolute.

"I was right," Kael said, and for the first time, her voice wavered. "About the threat. I was right, and I still looked exactly like the monster you were afraid of. And I don't—I don't know how to measure the distance between those two things anymore."

---

Saya didn't offer a platitude. Instead, she sat down on the dusty floor beside her.

"I thought you were broken," Saya said. Her voice was a whisper, stripped of her title. "I thought—the experiments, everything they did to you—I thought something had finally given way. That I was finally seeing the cracks."

The room went very still. Kael’s hands moved to her face, her palms pressing hard against her eyes as if trying to hold her thoughts inside her head.

"I know you didn't mean it that way," Kael said, her voice tight. The iron control she had carried her entire life was visibly fracturing. She pressed the back of her wrist against her eyes, a jagged, defensive motion.

"I'm sorry," Saya said.

They sat with the weight of the apology for a long time. The relay station seemed to hold the silence around them, shielding them from the rest of the world.

"You saved my life," Saya said. 

She said it simply, without the ceremony of the throne, into the quiet of the room.

Kael’s hand dropped. She looked at her sister, then quickly away, toward the scarred metal of the wall. She looked as though she had been braced for a blow and was instead handed a weight she had no idea how to carry.

"In the plaza," Saya continued. "I would have been dead before the guard even saw the flash. You saved me."

Kael didn't look back. She stared at the shadows. "I did what I was supposed to do."

---

"I don't trust myself around you right now," Kael said finally, the words coming out like a confession.

Saya looked at her, waiting.

"Not because I would hurt you. I mean—you are the reason I come back. Every time. You are the thing I hold onto. If I come back and I'm still not knowing if what I see can be trusted... I don't want to damage the only thing that has ever brought me home."

---

Saya’s eyes drifted back to the gold on Voruna. She looked at the scored, lightless trim.

"You waited outside for me once," she said.

Kael turned her head slightly.

"The Gold Anchor Failure. I stood outside your door for a long time before I came in. I have thought about that moment every single day since."

Kael said nothing, her breath hitching.

"I came in anyway." Saya held her gaze, her eyes fierce. "That's what I'm telling you. I came in anyway."

---

Something shifted in Kael’s posture. A loosening of the shoulders, a slight drop in the defensive tilt of her head.

"Give me three days," Kael said.

Saya looked at the damaged frame one last time, then at her sister. She nodded.

"Three days."

She stood up and walked out, her footsteps fading into the hollow depths of the station.

---

Kael sat alone in the empty relay station.

She didn't look at the file anymore. She thought about the gold. About what it said when it was intact, and what Saya had just told her.

*I came in.*

She had not known, until this moment, that the anchor was a choice her sister made. Every single time.

She finished the file in two days, working through the nights by the dim glow of her HUD. On the third morning, she stood up and looked at Voruna. The gold was still damaged, the edges still jagged and dull.

She picked up the frame, slung it over her shoulder, and began the long walk home.

She would fix the gold when she got there.
